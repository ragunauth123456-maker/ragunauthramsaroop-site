# From Climate Data to Cash Flow

A decision-grade framework for sustainability, risk-adjusted growth and credible financial performance

Ragunauth Ramsaroop | Independent white paper | September 2026

> GitHub editorial overview. The complete research manuscript and publication PDF form separate deliverables. This overview does not pretend to be the full manuscript. The paper was independently written after viewing a McKinsey social media post; no affiliation or endorsement is implied.

Sustainability is neither automatically a cost nor automatically a growth opportunity. Outcomes depend on physical performance, asset exposure, capital requirements, credible verification and the distribution of costs. This independent paper moves climate information from a reporting exercise into auditable operating and capital-allocation decisions.

## Original contribution: Four-Ledger Climate Value Model

**Physical truth** records energy, fuel, water, waste, exposure and service continuity with traceable measurement. **Financial effects** reconcile an explicit counterfactual with actual and scenario cash flows. **Claims** establish accounting boundaries, assurance, credible emissions inventory reductions and treatment of credits. **Distribution** identifies who pays, who benefits, and who bears residual effects.

## Issues examined

- Why installed renewable capacity does not prove generation, avoided diesel, net savings or certified emissions reduction.
- A climate-adjusted operating margin bridge covering efficiency, avoided interruption, adaptation expenditure, customer demand and financing terms.
- Flood, drought and correlated infrastructure risks, including insurance, recovery time and banking exposure.
- Project-specific implications for mining, utilities, agriculture and food processing.
- Governance roles for the CFO, engineering, sustainability, procurement, risk, community relations and independent audit.

## Evidence and use

The full manuscript includes financial scenario sensitivities with explicitly fictional assumptions, a board dashboard, a 12-month implementation sequence, a claims-control protocol and transparent limitations. It does not present hypothetical carbon prices, lending discounts or avoided losses as observed returns.
