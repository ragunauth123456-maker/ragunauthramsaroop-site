## 39. Extractive disclosure as an operating system

Transparency in a petroleum state has little value if disclosure arrives too late, uses incompatible definitions or cannot be reconciled across agencies. The strongest disclosure architecture therefore behaves as an operating system rather than a periodic communications exercise. It links contracts, production, government entitlement, royalties, audited recoverable costs, fund deposits, investment income, parliamentary withdrawals and public expenditure through shared dates and definitions.

Guyana's current public record contains several layers. The Petroleum Management Programme publishes petroleum agreements, block information, production licences, cost-audit documents, environmental records and a production dashboard. The Bank of Guyana publishes Natural Resource Fund reports and audited statements. The Ministry of Finance publishes budget and mid-year fiscal reports. Guyana EITI publishes national reports and participates in the international EITI validation process. These sources create a substantial documentary trail, but the trail still requires reconciliation by date and transaction. [S18, S33-S34, S41-S47, S69-S72, S81-S86]

The EITI process deserves separate treatment because it tests more than numerical publication. In May 2026, the EITI Board launched a full validation of Guyana from 15 May and recorded concern about continuing stakeholder confidence in the selection of civil-society representatives. The International Secretariat also sought views on stakeholder engagement and barriers affecting participation. At the evidence cut-off used for this manuscript, a final 2026 validation result had not been established. The proper research treatment is therefore to describe the process as ongoing, record the Board's stated concern and avoid substituting an author's political conclusion for an unfinished institutional review. [S16-S18, S33-S34, S69-S70]

The design lesson for an emerging producer is to separate disclosure into at least six ledgers. The first is a legal ledger containing executed contracts, licences, permits and amendments. The second is a production ledger containing measured output, cargoes and entitlement. The third is a revenue ledger containing royalties, profit oil, taxes, penalties and settlement dates. The fourth is a cost ledger containing recoverable expenditures, audit adjustments and dispute status. The fifth is a sovereign-fund ledger containing opening cash, deposits, investment returns, withdrawals and closing cash. The sixth is a public-investment ledger containing appropriations, tenders, awards, variations, completion, operation and maintenance. A seventh social and environmental ledger is warranted when the country has major local-content, community, biodiversity or climate-finance obligations.

A high-quality transparency platform should not force users to infer equivalence among these ledgers. For example, a monthly production volume does not imply a same-month government cash receipt. A disputed cost item does not imply a final cash recovery. An NRF withdrawal does not imply a completed road or hospital. A carbon-credit agreement does not imply a cash receipt in the petroleum fund. These distinctions protect public understanding and improve external audit.

## 40. Cost audits and revenue assurance

Cost recovery is one of the least intuitive components of a production-sharing system. Under the Stabroek framework, the economic effect of recoverable expenditure depends on the cost ceiling, the stock of eligible costs and the timing of production. The state's ability to verify cost claims therefore affects the timing and amount of profit oil even when headline contract terms remain unchanged.

Guyana's public petroleum portal includes an IHS Markit audit covering the 1999-2017 period and a VHE audit record for 2018-2020. The IHS Markit final report states that the audited cost-recovery submission totalled USD 1.6778 billion and identified USD 214.4 million plus overhead adjustments as having grounds for dispute. The Ministry of Natural Resources later stated that aspects of the earlier audit had moved into the production-sharing agreement's sole-expert procedure and described the status of several audit exercises. An audit exception, a government challenge, an expert determination and cash ultimately recovered are four different events. The flagship paper will preserve those statuses separately. [S42-S43, S71-S72]

For a new producer, the audit function should begin before first revenue. The state needs contract accountants, petroleum engineers, reservoir specialists, transfer-pricing expertise, legal counsel and access to source documentation. Cost categories should be mapped to contractual clauses before operators begin submitting large development claims. Data retention must be long enough to support later disputes. Audit timetables should account for the fact that offshore development projects spend billions before production begins, creating a large initial cost bank.

The revenue-assurance model should contain a claim register with fields for the cost category, project, invoice date, related party, contractual basis, amount claimed, amount accepted, amount challenged, audit reason, operator response, dispute stage, final determination and cash effect. Without these fields, public reporting risks compressing a multi-year accounting process into one headline number.

Audit independence also requires procurement safeguards. If the government hires external auditors, the public should know how the firm was selected, whether conflicts were screened, which years and entities fall within scope, and whether the contract permits access to underlying technical records. The state should maintain its own institutional memory rather than outsourcing the entire audit function to successive consulting teams.

## 41. Procurement and spending oversight

A petroleum windfall expands the public capital programme faster than administrative capacity may expand. Procurement oversight therefore becomes part of petroleum governance even when procurement law sits outside the petroleum sector. A country may account perfectly for oil revenue entering a sovereign fund and still convert the revenue poorly into durable assets.

Guyana's Ministry of Finance has published large public-investment allocations in successive budgets. IMF staff in 2026 highlighted absorption, project monitoring, procurement and public-enterprise oversight as policy priorities. The Audit Office publishes public-accounts and performance reports, including the 2024 public-accounts report presented to the National Assembly in September 2025. These records create a basis for project-level testing rather than broad claims about public-sector performance. [S64-S65, S73]

The flagship methodology therefore applies an outcome chain to major capital projects. The first stage is policy justification. The second is feasibility and lifecycle costing. The third is procurement and contractor selection. The fourth is construction and contract variation. The fifth is commissioning. The sixth is operation and maintenance. The seventh is measured service outcome. A project should not move to the final category because a contract was signed or because budgeted expenditure was executed.

For an emerging producer, the public-investment register should identify the original cost estimate, financing source, procurement route, evaluation criteria, winning bidder, beneficial owner, variation orders, completion date, maintenance provision, responsible operating agency and service indicators. Where climate exposure matters, the register should record flood, heat, hurricane, coastal or seismic design assumptions.

This framework also helps limit procyclical spending. If capital projects are approved faster than engineers, construction firms, ports, aggregate supply or procurement teams can deliver them, the result may be higher costs rather than proportionally higher public-service output. The correct test is not whether the government has the cash. The test is whether the economy and institutions can convert that cash into durable capacity at acceptable cost.

## 42. Parliament, judiciary and institutional checks

A resource-governance system is more resilient when multiple institutions can test decisions using different legal mandates. Parliament authorizes laws and public expenditure. Courts interpret legal obligations and review administrative action. Auditors examine financial records. Regulators issue permits and licences. Central banks hold or manage financial assets. EITI processes add a multi-stakeholder disclosure layer. None of these institutions performs the same function.

Guyana's petroleum framework provides several examples. Parliament enacted the 2019 Natural Resource Fund Act before first oil, later replaced it with the 2021 framework and amended withdrawal provisions in 2024. Parliament also enacted the Local Content Act 2021 and the Oil Pollution Prevention, Preparedness, Response and Responsibility Act 2025. Each statute needs a versioned legal crosswalk because enactment, assent, publication, commencement and implementing regulations do not necessarily occur on the same date. [S05, S19, S37, S53-S54, S57]

The judiciary has also become part of petroleum governance. The May 2026 Court of Appeal dispute over financial assurance illustrates why the final publication must use authenticated judgments rather than relying on a press release or one party's summary. The government stated that the appeal court set aside the earlier High Court requirement for an unlimited guarantee and confirmed regulatory discretion over the amount of financial assurance. This work treats that statement as an attributed description pending full judgment review. It does not transform a contested legal question into an authorial verdict. [S20]

The lesson for a new producer is procedural. Every major decision should identify the statutory authority, decision-maker, evidence considered, public notice requirement, reasons, appeal route, judicial-review route and record-retention requirement. Where a regulator possesses discretion, the file should preserve the reasons for exercising that discretion. This improves the ability of courts and auditors to review the decision later without requiring them to re-create the technical record from fragments.

Institutional checks work best when their boundaries are explicit. A court does not run an environmental agency. An environmental regulator does not set the annual budget. A finance ministry should not certify its own external audit. A sovereign-fund board should not replace parliament's appropriation role. The operating model should therefore map both authority and limits.

## 43. Statistical institutions and public data

A resource boom changes the statistical system as much as it changes the fiscal system. Production, exports, government revenue, construction, wages, migration, prices and household consumption can move quickly. If statistical releases lag, public debate relies on nominal spending, operator announcements or national GDP, none of which answers the full distributional question.

Guyana's Bureau of Statistics completed the 2018/2019 Household Budget Survey and Living Conditions work across all ten regions. The World Bank and IMF have both noted limits in the availability of more recent household information. IMF reports in 2025 and 2026 also discuss statistical modernization and data gaps affecting analysis of the non-oil economy and household conditions. The responsible approach is to date every welfare statistic and avoid describing a 2019 poverty observation as a 2026 outcome. [S10, S61, S64, S68]

A flagship resource-governance dashboard should therefore carry three dates for each indicator: observation period, release date and last revision. It should also identify geographic coverage, sample size where relevant, price base, currency, nominal or real status and source owner. Petroleum data need equally careful definitions. Monthly barrels, daily peaks, nameplate capacity, government lifts and cash receipts should never occupy one undifferentiated column.

The public-data system should include a corrections policy. Resource states experience frequent revisions because cargo prices settle after lifting, cost claims change through audit, national accounts undergo rebasing, and surveys improve. Publishing a revised series with a version history is better than defending an outdated number for reputational reasons.

The most useful indicator architecture for the final paper will include petroleum output, non-oil output, government revenue, NRF flows, inflation, employment, real wages, household consumption, poverty or deprivation, school and health outcomes, regional service access, public debt, construction costs, exchange-rate conditions, imports, exports outside petroleum and environmental performance. The point is not to produce one composite score. The point is to prevent a high petroleum-growth number from substituting for evidence on other outcomes.

## Institutional learning cycle

The final accountability model uses a six-stage loop.

1. **Decide.** A legally authorized institution makes a decision using a dated record.
2. **Disclose.** The underlying rule, assumptions and non-confidential evidence are published.
3. **Measure.** Actual production, cash, cost, environmental or service outcomes are recorded.
4. **Challenge.** Auditors, parliament, courts, stakeholders and independent experts test the evidence.
5. **Correct.** Errors, weak controls or outdated rules are revised with reasons.
6. **Archive.** The version history remains accessible so later analysts can distinguish what was known at each date.

This cycle is more transferable than any single petroleum contract or fund-withdrawal rule. It allows a new producer to learn without assuming that the first institutional design is permanent.

## Part VIII conclusions

Guyana's public record shows a growing network of petroleum disclosure, fund reporting, audit material, parliamentary lawmaking, court review and EITI engagement. The same record also contains unresolved audit disputes, an ongoing EITI validation at the research cut-off, legal questions requiring primary judgment review and statistical gaps affecting social analysis. These facts belong together.

The blueprint value lies in the structure of verification. A new producer should design for reconciliation, version control, challenge and correction from the start. Institutional maturity is not the absence of disputes. It is the ability to record them, resolve them through lawful procedures and preserve evidence of what changed.
