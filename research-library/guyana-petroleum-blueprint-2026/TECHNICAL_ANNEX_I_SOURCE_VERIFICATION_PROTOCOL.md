# TECHNICAL ANNEX I
## Source-to-claim verification, corrections, uncertainty and publication integrity protocol

Purpose: define the evidentiary rules used in the flagship publication so readers can reproduce major claims and distinguish verified facts from forecasts, interpretations and unresolved questions.

## I1. Claim classes

Every material statement should be classified internally as one of:
- legal fact;
- contractual fact;
- observed operational fact;
- audited financial fact;
- official administrative report;
- operator report;
- multilateral analysis;
- stakeholder position;
- forecast;
- model output;
- author synthesis.

The class determines the required source and wording.

## I2. Legal claims

Legal claims require the enacted text, official gazette, regulation, licence, permit or authenticated judgment where available.

A ministry press release describing a judgment is secondary evidence for the legal proposition. It may establish the ministry's interpretation but not replace the judgment.

## I3. Contract claims

Contract terms should come from the executed agreement or official published contract.

Model agreements belong in a separate category. They should not be applied retroactively to an older executed contract.

## I4. Financial claims

Priority order:
1. audited financial statement;
2. central-bank or treasury transaction report;
3. official budget or mid-year account;
4. operator statement where it concerns operator finances;
5. media only when primary records are unavailable.

If an audited account later restates an earlier number, the restated number should be used with a note.

## I5. Production claims

Use regulator data for actual production where available.

Operator announcements are acceptable for first-oil dates and milestones, but a stated capacity or daily record should not be described as annual average production.

## I6. Social claims

Use recent representative surveys for household outcomes.

Administrative programme counts may support statements about participation or spending, but not household welfare unless linked to outcome evidence.

## I7. Environmental claims

A permit establishes authorization and conditions.
An inspection establishes regulator observation.
Monitoring data establish measured environmental variables.
An enforcement order establishes a regulatory finding.
None alone proves overall environmental performance.

## I8. Carbon-finance claims

Separate:
- methodology approval;
- verified units;
- issuance;
- sale contract;
- transfer;
- cash receipt;
- community allocation;
- expenditure;
- outcome.

The minimum contract value is not the same as cash received.

## I9. Evidence confidence

Use three internal labels:
**High:** primary authoritative record with clear date and definition.
**Moderate:** reliable official or independent analysis with some limitation.
**Unresolved:** conflicting sources, missing primary document or material ambiguity.

The public paper should explain uncertainty rather than display a hidden numerical score.

## I10. Observation date

Every time-sensitive figure needs:
- observation period;
- publication date;
- evidence cut-off.

This is essential for 2026 production, fund balances and EITI status.

## I11. Forecast wording

Use:
- projected;
- targeted;
- planned;
- estimated;
- operator expects;
- government projects.

Do not use:
- will produce;
- will receive;
- completed;
unless observed evidence supports it.

## I12. Causality

Avoid causal claims from temporal association alone.

Example:
Petroleum revenue and public construction may rise together, but a specific welfare outcome requires a plausible mechanism and evidence.

## I13. Political claims

Where a source criticizes or supports a political decision, attribute the view.

The author should describe evidence, institutional process and policy effects without endorsing or opposing a party, official or electoral choice.

## I14. Quote control

Use short quotations only when wording matters legally or analytically.

Most source material should be paraphrased with a citation.

## I15. Source register fields

Each source record should include:
- ID;
- author/institution;
- title;
- date;
- source type;
- jurisdiction;
- claim supported;
- page/section;
- URL;
- access date;
- limitation;
- superseded status.

## I16. Claim register

For each high-impact numerical claim:
- claim ID;
- manuscript location;
- value;
- unit;
- date;
- source ID;
- calculation;
- reviewer;
- status.

This allows automated checking.

## I17. Cross-source reconciliation

Where ministry, central bank and IMF figures differ:
1. compare period;
2. compare currency;
3. compare cash/accrual;
4. compare revision date;
5. compare scope;
6. use the most appropriate source and explain the difference.

Do not average incompatible figures.

## I18. Contradictory evidence

A contradiction register should preserve:
- source A;
- source B;
- disputed proposition;
- likely reason;
- unresolved status;
- final treatment.

Deleting inconvenient sources reduces credibility.

## I19. Corrections

A corrections register should show:
- date identified;
- original text;
- corrected text;
- reason;
- affected pages;
- source;
- version released.

Material corrections should remain visible in the digital archive.

## I20. Versioning

Use version numbers:
- research draft;
- review draft;
- publication 1.0;
- corrected 1.1.

Every PDF should carry:
- version;
- evidence cut-off;
- SHA-256;
- publication date.

## I21. External review

External review should identify:
- reviewer expertise;
- conflicts;
- scope;
- date;
- comments;
- author response.

A paper should never imply peer review when only internal checking occurred.

## I22. Fiscal model review

Review:
- contract interpretation;
- units;
- price;
- production;
- cost recovery;
- tax;
- timing;
- discounting.

A model should be reproducible from disclosed inputs.

## I23. Environmental review

Review:
- permit version;
- baseline;
- monitoring;
- spill assumptions;
- liability;
- insurance;
- transboundary risk.

Environmental review should not be limited to climate emissions.

## I24. Social review

Review:
- survey year;
- sample;
- regional coverage;
- Indigenous/community evidence;
- distribution;
- programme versus outcome.

## I25. Comparator review

Review whether comparator data use common dates and definitions.

A mature producer's current fund balance should not be compared directly with a new producer's first-year cash without context.

## I26. Repetition audit

A 500-plus-page publication risks repeated narrative.

The final build should detect repeated long paragraphs and flag identical text. Technical annexes may restate definitions when needed, but repeated prose should be minimized.

## I27. Page-substance audit

Page count is not a quality metric by itself.

The final audit should report:
- total pages;
- pages with at least 100 words;
- figures/tables;
- source pages;
- blank pages;
- duplicate paragraphs.

A page with a full analytical table may be substantive despite fewer than 100 words and should be identified separately.

## I28. Link audit

Every URL should be checked for:
- response;
- authoritative domain;
- correct document;
- date;
- version.

Archived copies should be used where lawful and appropriate for fragile links.

## I29. PDF audit

Check:
- searchable text;
- bookmarks;
- page numbers;
- TOC;
- metadata;
- broken glyphs;
- clipping;
- table overflow;
- citation legibility;
- file checksum.

## I30. Publication decision

The paper is ready for a publication edition when:
- all material claims have source IDs;
- unresolved material issues are disclosed;
- models reproduce;
- legal versions are correct;
- final PDF passes visual and structural QA.

External peer review is an additional status, not a prerequisite to honestly publishing independent research if its absence is stated.

## I31. Annex conclusion

Publication integrity depends on traceability and correction. The value of the Guyana case study will come from readers being able to distinguish what happened, what was projected, what remains disputed and how the evidence was checked.
