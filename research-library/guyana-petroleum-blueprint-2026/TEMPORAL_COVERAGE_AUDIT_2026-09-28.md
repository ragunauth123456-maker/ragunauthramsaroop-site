# Temporal Coverage Audit - Past, Present and Future

Date: 28 September 2026  
Scope: Uploaded Edition 1.3 plus the current GitHub research branch for Guyana's Petroleum Transformation.  
Purpose: Ensure the definitive publication tells one continuous, evidence-led story from petroleum prehistory through the current 2026 position and into clearly labelled future scenarios.

## Audit conclusion

Edition 1.3 is not yet the definitive LinkedIn/publication edition. It is materially stronger than Edition 1.2, but its 41-page integration supplement remains attached ahead of the unchanged 491-page earlier edition rather than being fully integrated into one narrative. The current PDF also underuses newer research already present on this GitHub branch.

The final edition should be rebuilt from the strongest integrated branch material, not by appending another supplement.

## 1. Past - required historical arc

The final publication should explicitly cover and date:

- early petroleum indications and the first oil prospection licence recorded in 1938;
- renewed offshore exploration activity recorded from 1965;
- the 1982-1993 World Bank-supported Petroleum Exploration Promotion Project, including the Petroleum Unit/capacity-building dimension, project-credit utilization evidence and its limitations;
- the Petroleum (Exploration and Production) Act 1986 and the legal framework inherited before Liza;
- the Environmental Protection Act 1996;
- maritime and boundary history relevant to offshore petroleum governance;
- the pre-oil commodity economy and institutional baseline, including sugar, bauxite, gold, forestry, public finance, electricity, infrastructure and state capacity;
- the 2009 Low Carbon Development Strategy and Guyana-Norway forest partnership as pre-petroleum institutions;
- the exploration/licensing sequence leading into the 2015 Liza discovery.

The uploaded Edition 1.3 contains 1938, 1965, 1986, 1996 and 2009 references, but it does not contain the 1982 or 1993 archival milestones now present in the Edition 2 research branch. This is a material historical gap.

## 2. Present - current position at the 27 September 2026 evidence cut-off

The final publication should preserve a dated 2015-2026 operating chronology and distinguish observed facts from forecasts:

- May 2015 Liza discovery;
- 27 June 2016 Stabroek agreement and 7 October 2016 effective-date distinction;
- environmental and development approvals before first oil;
- January 2019 NRF legislation before December 2019 first oil;
- Liza Phase 1 first oil, December 2019;
- Liza Phase 2, February 2022;
- Payara, November 2023;
- Yellowtail, August 2025;
- 2021 Local Content Act and replacement NRF framework;
- Petroleum Activities Act 2023;
- 2024 NRF withdrawal-framework changes;
- Oil Pollution Act 2025 and the difference between gazetting, commencement and implementing regulations;
- 2026 production, government lifts, royalties, profit-oil receipts and NRF position using consistent cash/accrual definitions;
- 2026 EITI Validation status, IMF staff findings, NDC 3.0, LCDS 2030 UNFCCC submission and environmental/transparency updates;
- project-level environmental permits, financial assurance, cost-audit status, local-content evidence and current social/outcome-data limitations.

Open present-period controls that must remain visible:
- exact first-half 2026 NRF cash/accrual reconciliation, including the approximately US$184.23 million recognition bridge identified in the research branch;
- unresolved disposition of petroleum cost-audit exceptions;
- authenticated legal/financial-assurance evidence where full instruments are not public;
- stronger current household, Indigenous/community and domestic-value-added outcome evidence.

## 3. Future - concrete Guyana forward path plus scenarios

Edition 1.3 contains general future manuals and scenarios, but the definitive publication needs a much clearer Guyana-specific forward chapter.

The current GitHub offshore project register provides the minimum project pipeline:

- Uaru - Errea Wittu FPSO, 250,000 bpd design capacity, about US$12.7 billion project cost, commissioning/no first-oil confirmation at the 27 September 2026 cut-off.
- Whiptail - Jaguar FPSO, 250,000 bpd design capacity, about US$12.7 billion project cost, under development with startup targeted for 2027.
- Hammerhead - approved/FID 22 September 2025, about 150,000 bpd design capacity, about US$6.8 billion project cost, startup anticipated for 2029.

Hammerhead is absent from the uploaded Edition 1.3 and should be added.

The future section should then separate three horizons:

### 2026-2030 - buildout
- Uaru, Whiptail and Hammerhead commissioning/startup;
- regulator staffing and audit scaling;
- production, cargo and NRF accounting capacity;
- Gas-to-Energy and grid-delivery outcomes;
- public-investment execution and maintenance;
- local-content capability expansion;
- cumulative environmental and spill-response capacity.

### 2030s - maturity and national conversion
- production plateau/decline scenarios rather than one deterministic forecast;
- oil-price and downtime stress tests;
- NRF saving/withdrawal sustainability;
- non-oil primary balance and debt management;
- diversification, productivity, workforce and competitiveness;
- infrastructure lifecycle costs;
- emissions, climate commitments and natural-capital accounting.

### 2040s and end-of-life
- field decline;
- well abandonment and subsea removal;
- FPSO exit;
- decommissioning funding/security;
- environmental restoration and monitoring;
- residual liability;
- transition of petroleum skills and suppliers into other sectors;
- fiscal resilience after peak petroleum revenues.

Edition 1.3 contains no explicit 2040 horizon. A definitive national transformation study should include one, clearly marked as scenario analysis rather than prediction.

## 4. Structural correction

The final public edition should not contain:
- a new supplement followed by an unchanged older document;
- repeated versions of the same chronology;
- internal language such as "candidate", "final paper should", "remaining evidence gates" throughout the main narrative;
- research-dossier instructions inside reader-facing chapters;
- duplicate analytical sections that restate the same point.

The public-facing structure should be one continuous manuscript:

1. Executive synthesis and methodology
2. Guyana before petroleum
3. Exploration and institutional prehistory
4. Discovery and contract formation
5. Pre-first-oil institution building
6. First oil and production scale-up
7. Petroleum revenue, NRF and public finance
8. Environmental governance and cumulative offshore risk
9. Local content, households, communities and social outcomes
10. Economic transformation, infrastructure and energy
11. Governance, audit, EITI and accountability
12. Guyana 2026 - current national position
13. Guyana 2026-2030 - sanctioned buildout
14. Guyana in the 2030s - maturity, diversification and fiscal resilience
15. Guyana in the 2040s - decline, decommissioning and post-petroleum resilience
16. International comparisons and limits of transfer
17. New-producer operating manual
18. Technical annexes, data, source register and corrections record

## 5. Release standard

The definitive publication should be released only after:
- the historical gaps above are integrated;
- Uaru, Whiptail and Hammerhead are included in one dated project register;
- every forecast is labelled as forecast/scenario;
- present-period financial data are reconciled to a consistent accounting basis;
- duplicated Edition 1.3 material is removed;
- internal drafting instructions are removed from the reader-facing manuscript;
- full PDF render/visual QA passes;
- bookmarks, TOC, page numbering and source links are checked;
- the cover and metadata no longer describe the public release as a working or scrutiny candidate.

This audit is a temporal-completeness and publication-architecture control. It does not replace independent specialist review of legal, fiscal, environmental, social or petroleum-engineering claims.
