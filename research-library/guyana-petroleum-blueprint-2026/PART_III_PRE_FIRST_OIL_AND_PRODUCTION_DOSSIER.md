# Research objective

Part III reconstructs the interval from commercial discovery through large-scale production.

The analytical sequence is:

commercial discovery  
→ appraisal  
→ development approval  
→ environmental authorization  
→ production licence  
→ project construction  
→ first oil  
→ production ramp  
→ cargo lifting  
→ government entitlement  
→ petroleum cash receipt.

Each stage has a different date and a different source.

The final publication will avoid a common error: using a single "first oil" date as if it represented the full development and revenue cycle.

# Chapter 12 research dossier
## First oil and the first revenue cycle

Production from Liza Phase 1 began in December 2019. Official and operator records consistently place the first project startup in that month. [S81, S83]

First production is not first government cash.

A cargo-based accounting chain should include:
1. production measured on the FPSO;
2. crude stored;
3. cargo nominated;
4. entitlement assigned;
5. tanker lifted;
6. cargo priced;
7. invoice settled;
8. royalty/profit-oil receipt recognized;
9. money deposited in the NRF.

The 2020 audited NRF statements show the practical timing difference. The fund recognized four 2020 profit-oil lifts while the fourth was still a receivable at year end and settled in 2021. [S45]

This makes the first-year case useful for newcomer countries.

A petroleum ministry, revenue authority, central bank and finance ministry must agree on:
- the production period;
- lift date;
- price;
- entitlement;
- invoice date;
- settlement date;
- accounting year.

Without this bridge, public reports can contain different numbers that are each correct under different accounting bases.

# Chapter 14 research dossier
## Production data quality

Production data should be stored at the finest reliable time resolution available.

The core dataset needs:
- date;
- field/project;
- FPSO;
- oil produced;
- water produced;
- gas produced;
- gas reinjected;
- gas flared;
- downtime;
- oil lifted.

## Five measures that must not be confused

### Nameplate/design capacity
Engineering production capability under specified conditions.

### Daily production
Observed output on a particular day.

### Monthly daily average
Monthly barrels divided by days.

### Annual average
Annual barrels divided by 365/366.

### Cargo lift
A shipment event that draws from stored produced crude and need not match the same month's production.

A headline such as "900,000 barrels per day" must therefore identify whether it is:
- installed capacity;
- target;
- one-day milestone;
- period average.

# Production and revenue bridge

The flagship should build a reconciled table with three linked but distinct layers.

## Layer A. Physical production
- monthly barrels by FPSO;
- total field production;
- downtime.

## Layer B. Commercial lifting
- cargo count;
- barrels lifted;
- entitlement owner;
- lift date;
- price.

## Layer C. Public cash
- government profit-oil invoice;
- royalty;
- settlement date;
- NRF deposit.

The bridge will explain timing differences instead of forcing the three layers into the same calendar month.

# Production-scale regulatory pressure

Production growth also changes administrative workload.

Each additional project creates:
- production reporting;
- cost statements;
- environmental monitoring;
- permit review;
- spill-response exposure;
- decommissioning obligations;
- local-content plans;
- cargo sales;
- tax filings.

The flagship should compare regulatory staffing and audit resources against:
- number of FPSOs;
- number of active wells;
- production volume;
- annual contractor spend.

This is a more meaningful capacity test than the number of laws enacted.
