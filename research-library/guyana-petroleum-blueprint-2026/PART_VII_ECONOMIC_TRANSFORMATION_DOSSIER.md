# PART VII RESEARCH DOSSIER
## Economic Transformation, Public Investment, Energy, Diversification and Competitiveness

Working research dossier for Chapters 34-38 of the 520-560-page flagship publication.  
Evidence cut-off: 27 September 2026.  
Status: research stage. Macroeconomic and public-investment datasets require further reconciliation.

# Research objective

Part VII asks whether petroleum income is being converted into durable productive capacity.

The analytical frame is broader than GDP.

The chapter separates:
- oil output;
- non-oil output;
- public investment;
- private investment;
- household welfare;
- infrastructure service quality;
- financial savings;
- human capital;
- natural capital;
- competitiveness.

No single indicator answers the development question.

# Chapter 34 research dossier
## Growth beyond headline GDP

IMF staff reported in July 2026 that real GDP grew by more than 19% in 2025 after average growth near 40% during 2023-2024. Staff also described broad non-oil expansion and similar high petroleum production during the first half of 2026. [S64]

These IMF observations provide a macroeconomic snapshot, but the final chapter should use official national accounts and revised historical series for quantitative decomposition.

## GDP decomposition

Required time series:
- total real GDP;
- oil GDP;
- non-oil GDP;
- construction;
- agriculture;
- mining;
- manufacturing;
- transport;
- services;
- government;
- per-capita measures.

The dataset should preserve base-year changes and statistical revisions.

## Why oil GDP requires separate treatment

Petroleum production can increase national output rapidly while:
- employing a small share of the labour force directly;
- importing substantial specialized equipment and services;
- generating profits accruing to foreign investors;
- generating government revenue on a different timetable.

This does not make oil GDP irrelevant. It means GDP must be linked to national income, fiscal receipts, domestic value added and household outcomes.

## Non-oil growth

The final chapter should test:
- whether non-oil growth is concentrated in construction;
- whether tradable sectors expand;
- whether private productivity rises;
- whether public investment reduces logistics and power constraints;
- whether sector growth persists when oil-linked construction moderates.

## Labour market

The IMF July 2026 statement reports unemployment at 6.2% at end-2025 and notes labour shortages as a policy issue. [S64]

The flagship should pair unemployment with:
- participation;
- vacancies;
- wage growth;
- migration;
- occupation;
- public/private sector;
- underemployment.

# Chapter 35 research dossier
## Infrastructure transformation

Petroleum revenue increases fiscal capacity, but infrastructure conversion has several stages:

appropriation  
→ procurement  
→ contract  
→ construction  
→ commissioning  
→ operation  
→ maintenance  
→ service outcome.

The final project register should never mark an allocation as a completed asset.

## Infrastructure classes

### Transport
- national roads;
- bridges;
- community roads;
- hinterland roads;
- ports;
- airports.

### Water and drainage
- potable water;
- drainage;
- canals;
- pumps;
- sea defence.

### Social
- hospitals;
- schools;
- housing;
- digital infrastructure.

### Productive
- power generation;
- transmission;
- industrial sites;
- logistics facilities.

## Project register fields

For every major project:
- approved objective;
- implementing agency;
- original budget;
- revised budget;
- financing source;
- procurement method;
- contractor;
- award date;
- original completion date;
- actual completion;
- variations;
- maintenance plan;
- users;
- service performance.

This allows the paper to distinguish financial execution from delivery.

## Lifecycle cost

Large petroleum-financed capital programmes create future recurrent costs.

The national balance sheet should estimate:
- maintenance;
- staffing;
- energy;
- insurance;
- replacement;
- debt service where applicable.

A project that requires high future subsidies has a different wealth profile from a financial asset earning external returns.

# Chapter 36 research dossier
## Energy security and gas-to-energy choices

The 2026 Budget Speech describes a 300 MW first phase of the Gas-to-Energy project and a broader programme of generation and grid investment. [S65]

The Guyana Energy Agency describes natural gas as part of a transition in the integrated grid alongside solar, wind, hydro and other resources. [S67]

These sources state government/agency plans. The flagship should separately measure actual commissioning, delivered megawatt-hours, reliability, cost and emissions.

## Power-system metrics

Required:
- installed MW;
- dependable MW;
- peak demand;
- annual generation;
- system losses;
- outage frequency;
- outage duration;
- fuel consumption;
- fuel cost;
- average tariff;
- subsidy;
- renewable share;
- emissions intensity.

## Gas-to-energy evaluation

The final analysis should identify:
- offshore pipeline capital cost;
- onshore NGL/power capital cost;
- financing;
- gas supply arrangements;
- operating cost;
- plant efficiency;
- grid upgrades;
- outage assumptions;
- tariff policy;
- LPG output;
- emissions relative to displaced HFO/diesel;
- methane leakage;
- project delay risk.

A government statement that a project will reduce prices is a policy claim until observed tariffs and full system costs are available.

## Demand-side management

The GEA July 2026 update states the new action plan targets 516 GWh of energy savings by 2030 against projected generation of 2,508 GWh. [S66]

The flagship should track:
- implemented measures;
- verified savings;
- rebound effects;
- programme cost;
- household/business participation.

# Chapter 37 research dossier
## Private-sector development and diversification

Diversification should mean more than an increase in construction linked to petroleum.

The final chapter should test:
- export diversification;
- productivity;
- firm formation;
- domestic manufacturing;
- agriculture and food exports;
- services exports;
- mining value added;
- tourism;
- technology;
- regional trade.

## Diversification metrics

Possible metrics:
- non-oil export share;
- Herfindahl export concentration;
- non-oil productivity;
- manufacturing value added;
- food import dependence;
- firm survival;
- formal employment;
- private investment outside petroleum support;
- research and technical capacity.

The paper should avoid a single composite political score.

## Linkages

Petroleum can support non-oil sectors through:
- cheaper energy;
- logistics investment;
- skilled labour;
- demand;
- finance;
- public investment.

It can also create pressures through:
- higher wages;
- higher rents;
- construction demand;
- exchange-rate pressure;
- imported services;
- concentration of finance.

The net effect requires data by sector.

# Chapter 38 research dossier
## Inflation, competitiveness and Dutch-disease channels

IMF staff in July 2026 stated that available indicators did not show clear signs of overheating or resource-driven competitiveness pressure at that point, while noting that strong wage growth and wage-based real exchange rate indicators required monitoring. The same statement identified stronger foreign-exchange demand associated with import-intensive private investment. [S64]

These are dated IMF staff assessments, not permanent findings.

The flagship should construct its own indicator dashboard.

## Indicator set

- CPI overall;
- food inflation;
- housing/rent;
- land prices;
- construction materials;
- nominal wages;
- real wages;
- unit labour cost;
- real effective exchange rate;
- imports;
- non-oil exports;
- vacancy rates;
- construction prices;
- public procurement prices.

## Dutch-disease channels

The chapter should explain three mechanisms separately:

1. Spending effect: resource income increases domestic demand for non-tradables.
2. Resource movement: labour/capital shift toward petroleum-linked activity.
3. Exchange-rate/price effect: domestic costs rise relative to trading partners.

Guyana's stabilized exchange-rate framework changes how adjustment appears in observed indicators.

## Policy response framework

The paper should describe, without prescribing a political choice:
- saving more petroleum revenue;
- pacing public investment;
- raising productive capacity;
- importing scarce inputs;
- labour training;
- migration policy;
- competition policy;
- productivity investment;
- exchange-rate/monetary options.

Each has trade-offs.

# National wealth conversion framework

Petroleum extraction reduces an underground asset.

Long-run wealth conversion can be examined through changes in:
- sovereign financial assets;
- public infrastructure;
- human capital;
- private productive capital;
- environmental/natural capital;
- public debt;
- contingent liabilities.

A national wealth table should avoid double counting.

For example:
- petroleum revenue deposited into the NRF increases financial assets;
- a withdrawal reduces financial assets;
- a completed road increases produced capital;
- maintenance liabilities affect future public spending;
- environmental damage reduces natural capital.

The quality of wealth conversion depends on asset performance, not spending volume alone.

- strong construction;
- large public investment;
- increased FX demand with heavy import content;

The flagship should identify each as an IMF staff statement, not an independent fact where the claim is interpretive.

# New-producer blueprint extracted from Part VII

Before spending large resource revenue, a new producer should build:
- public-investment appraisal;
- procurement transparency;
- cost and schedule control;
- lifecycle maintenance budgeting;
- project commissioning verification;
- national accounts capable of separating resource and non-resource sectors;
- household price monitoring;
- labour-market data;
- infrastructure service indicators;
- national balance-sheet reporting.

A small island state should add:
- hurricane resilience;
- port redundancy;
- imported construction inflation;
- tourism competitiveness;
- desalination/power exposure;
- limited contractor capacity.

# Publication boundary

Part VII will not infer household prosperity from GDP growth.

It will not infer infrastructure performance from budget size.

It will not infer economic diversification from non-oil construction alone.

Each proposition requires its own evidence.
