# Guyana's Petroleum Transformation
## From exploration to production: institutions, environmental stewardship, wealth conversion and a transferable case-study framework
Author: Ragunauth Ramsaroop
Working research programme, opened 27 September 2026. Cut-off for observed facts: 27 September 2026.
Parent issue: https://github.com/ragunauth123456-maker/ragunauthramsaroop-site/issues/60

### Publication purpose
Explain the full sequence from early geological exploration to commercial discovery, contracts, first oil, a sovereign revenue fund, rapid production expansion, climate policy, local-content rules, environmental risk and public investment. Test what is replicable for another country, including islands with limited technical capacity, and what depends on Guyana's distinctive geology, forests, continental coastline, discoveries, finance, political and institutional history. A case study of documented decisions is not an endorsement of any party, official or policy. Distinguish verified results from proposals and government or operator claims.

### Research questions
1. Which milestones took place before the first commercial discovery, between discovery and first oil, and after production?
2. How did exploration licences and the 2016 Stabroek agreement allocate geological, capital, price, operational and environmental risks? What changed with the 2023 model terms?
3. Which provisions of the 2019 NRF law preceded first oil, which changed under the 2021 replacement and 2024 amendment, and how did actual cash balances and withdrawals evolve?
4. Are macroeconomic growth and actual household welfare moving together? Where do statistical age, geography and distribution prevent definitive conclusions?
5. How do licensing, EPA permits, EIA review, spill liability, insurance, preparedness, independent monitoring and community engagement work as a linked system? Distinguish enacted law from law in force.
6. What are the measurable effects and limits of local-content programmes, contract-cost audits, beneficial-ownership registers and EITI disclosure?
7. How does the petroleum industry coexist with the earlier 2009 low-carbon strategy, 2022 LCDS 2030, coastal resilience, biodiversity and forest-carbon finance?
8. Which design elements transfer to new producers, and under which legal, geologic, environmental, financing and institutional conditions do they fail?

### Evidence standard
Use directly linked laws, agreements, regulator permits, audited accounts, petroleum production records, published financial statements, EITI decisions, operator announcements, World Bank data, IMF reports, environmental judgments, methodology documents and independently documented stakeholder concerns. A government press release establishes what government announced, not the independent success of the announced programme. An operator forecast does not establish production achieved. An enacted act is not necessarily in force. A revenue deposit is not equivalent to investment return, budget spending or delivered social benefit. Do not treat a historical household poverty survey as current poverty. Record contradictory material and preserve an errata log.

### Proposed reproducible tests
- Oil at 40, 60 and 80 USD per barrel under transparent production and cost scenarios, illustrative only.
- Cost-recovery share at 25%, 50% and 75% for a simplified 2016 contractual revenue waterfall. Verify contract accounting before presenting as a fiscal take estimate.
- Fund balance under alternative price, spending and return assumptions. Never use an operator's forecast as a guaranteed revenue stream.
- Supply-chain effects net of imported services and local ownership, with data gaps explicitly reported.
- Spill stress tests: specified discharge volumes, transboundary currents, responder mobilization delay, liability layering and publicly funded tail risk. No unverified oil-spill dispersion model.

### Country-specific boundaries for transfer
- A small-island producer without large tropical forests cannot copy forest-carbon receipts, satellite deforestation accounting or Amerindian territorial institutions as-is.
- A small state with limited offshore depth or a different concession fiscal regime cannot copy Guyana's precise 2016 or 2023 fiscal terms without technical and legal adaptation.
- A state without a discovered commercial resource should not treat forecast petroleum receipts as a substitute for budget finance.
- Geological luck, access to offshore operators, regional supply chains, pre-existing law and port infrastructure are contextual facts, not administrative practices.
- Extract administrative decision gates and disclosure systems, then map resource endowment, ecological exposure, absorptive capacity, legal traditions and international obligations.
