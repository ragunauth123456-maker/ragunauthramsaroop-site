# TECHNICAL ANNEX E
## Local content, workforce, household welfare, regional distribution and social accountability

Purpose: define how to test whether petroleum activity creates durable domestic value and broad social gains. Source IDs refer to SOURCE_REGISTER.md.

## E1. Local content is not one number

Local content can refer to legal ownership, procurement, employment, wages, skills, domestic manufacturing, tax residence, retained profit or technology transfer. These are different measures.

Guyana's Local Content Act 2021 created a statutory framework and registration system. [S57-S58] Government mid-year reports describe procurement and employment activity in covered sectors. [S59-S60] The final paper should preserve the distinction between programme reporting and independently verified domestic value added.

## E2. Supplier classification

For each supplier, record:
- legal entity;
- beneficial owner;
- Guyanese ownership share;
- employees by residence;
- payroll in Guyana;
- imports;
- domestic purchases;
- local subcontractors;
- profit retained;
- tax paid;
- technical capability;
- safety certification;
- export sales.

A supplier registered locally but importing nearly all value has a different economic effect from a firm manufacturing or engineering locally.

## E3. Gross procurement versus domestic value

A procurement invoice may contain:
- imported equipment;
- freight;
- foreign specialist services;
- local labour;
- local materials;
- local margin;
- tax.

Only part of the invoice is domestic value added. The final methodology should estimate each component where data permit.

The formula is:
Domestic value added = local wages + local operating surplus + local taxes less subsidies + domestic intermediate value added.

This is conceptually different from gross spend.

## E4. Beneficial ownership

Local-content systems should verify beneficial ownership rather than rely solely on nominal shareholding.

The register should identify:
- natural persons with control;
- voting rights;
- related parties;
- management control;
- financing arrangements;
- changes in ownership.

Front-company risk rises when statutory local-content thresholds create economic rents.

## E5. Employment quality

Employment analysis should include:
- job count;
- occupation;
- contract status;
- wage;
- benefits;
- training;
- safety;
- duration;
- promotion;
- gender;
- region.

A temporary support job and a certified petroleum engineer are both employment, but their long-run productivity effects differ.

## E6. Skills pipeline

The skills strategy should map:
- required occupations;
- current supply;
- training duration;
- accreditation;
- employer demand;
- placement;
- retention.

Training should be evaluated by completion and employment outcome, not enrolment alone.

## E7. Public-sector talent competition

Petroleum expansion can draw engineers, lawyers, accountants and IT specialists from regulators and ministries.

The state should monitor:
- vacancy rates;
- turnover;
- wage gap;
- recruitment time;
- training cost;
- contractor secondments.

A successful local-content programme should not unintentionally weaken the regulator.

## E8. Immigration and specialist labour

A small workforce may require foreign specialists during rapid scale-up.

Policy choices should distinguish:
- temporary expertise;
- permanent migration;
- knowledge transfer;
- understudy arrangements;
- professional licensing.

The analysis should describe labour-market effects rather than present a political preference.

## E9. Safety and competence

Local participation targets should never lower safety requirements.

For safety-critical occupations, track:
- certification;
- hours of supervised practice;
- medical fitness;
- emergency training;
- incident record;
- refresher training.

Competence is a prerequisite to localization.

## E10. Supplier development

Supplier policy can focus on:
- finance;
- standards;
- quality systems;
- bidding;
- insurance;
- cybersecurity;
- environmental compliance;
- export readiness.

A grant or registration certificate should not be counted as commercial capability without contracts and performance.

## E11. Exportable capability

The strongest domestic value may come from services that can later export to Suriname, Trinidad and Tobago or other markets.

Potential examples include:
- logistics;
- environmental monitoring;
- engineering;
- safety training;
- fabrication;
- marine services;
- data;
- professional services.

The final paper should identify sectors based on evidence rather than assume all petroleum services are scalable.

## E12. Household welfare measurement

GDP growth is not a household welfare measure.

Use:
- real household consumption;
- income;
- poverty/deprivation;
- food security;
- housing;
- utilities;
- health;
- education;
- transport;
- digital access.

Guyana's latest cited household survey in the current source base covers 2018/2019. [S61] It should not be presented as a 2026 poverty estimate.

## E13. Survey freshness

Every social statistic should show:
- survey year;
- publication year;
- sample;
- regions;
- price base;
- methodology.

Where recent data do not exist, say so.

## E14. Distribution

National averages can conceal differences across:
- coast;
- hinterland;
- urban;
- rural;
- Indigenous villages;
- income groups;
- gender;
- age.

A petroleum case study should disaggregate when data permit.

## E15. Inflation and real income

Rapid investment can raise nominal wages and prices simultaneously.

The relevant measure is real purchasing power. Track:
- wage growth;
- food prices;
- rent;
- construction cost;
- transport;
- utilities.

A nominal wage increase may not improve welfare if living costs rise faster.

## E16. Housing

Resource booms often place pressure on land and housing.

Monitor:
- rent;
- home price;
- building permits;
- serviced lots;
- mortgage cost;
- informal housing;
- regional concentration.

Housing programmes should be tested through completed, occupied, serviced units.

## E17. Health

Petroleum revenue may fund health infrastructure, but outcomes depend on staff and operations.

Track:
- facility completion;
- doctors/nurses;
- equipment uptime;
- medicine availability;
- travel time;
- waiting time;
- mortality indicators.

A hospital building is not equivalent to a functioning service.

## E18. Education

Track:
- attendance;
- completion;
- learning;
- technical certification;
- tertiary graduation;
- employer placement.

Capital spending and scholarships are inputs.

## E19. Regional equity

A regional dashboard should compare service access before and after major petroleum-funded programmes.

Use consistent denominators and document data gaps. Per-capita spending alone does not indicate need or outcome.

## E20. Amerindian and hinterland evidence

LCDS materials describe village participation and a 15% community allocation from carbon-credit revenue. [S62-S63]

The independent research test should examine:
- village decision process;
- funds received;
- project selected;
- procurement;
- completion;
- maintenance;
- local satisfaction;
- grievance.

Government-reported participation should be triangulated with community sources where available.

## E21. Free, prior and informed participation

Where applicable law or policy requires consultation, the evidence record should include:
- notice;
- information;
- time for deliberation;
- representative authority;
- consent/decision;
- dissent;
- conditions;
- appeal.

Participation should not be inferred from attendance alone.

## E22. Grievance system

A grievance register should record:
- date;
- complainant category;
- issue;
- project;
- responsible body;
- acknowledgement;
- investigation;
- remedy;
- closure;
- appeal.

Personal information should be protected.

## E23. Social licence

"Social licence" is not a legal permit.

The paper should use the term only to describe stakeholder acceptance or relationship quality and should support claims with surveys, consultation records or documented stakeholder positions.

## E24. Gender

Petroleum labour markets can be male dominated.

Track:
- employment by occupation and gender;
- pay;
- training;
- leadership;
- contractor workforce;
- safety and harassment procedures.

The goal is evidence, not a generic equality statement.

## E25. Youth

Youth outcomes can be tracked through:
- training;
- apprenticeships;
- first employment;
- school-to-work transition;
- entrepreneurship.

Programme participation should be linked to employment and income where possible.

## E26. Small business finance

Petroleum suppliers often need working capital, insurance and equipment finance.

Track:
- loan approvals;
- cost of capital;
- payment terms;
- contract duration;
- default;
- procurement concentration.

A local-content target without finance may privilege already-capitalized firms.

## E27. Procurement concentration

Calculate:
- top five supplier share;
- top ten;
- related-party share;
- foreign-import component;
- local SME share.

High concentration is not automatically improper, especially in specialist services, but it deserves explanation.

## E28. Wage spillovers

Petroleum wage premiums may increase pay elsewhere or create staff shortages.

Track public service, construction, mining, hospitality and professional wages over time.

## E29. Migration

Rapid growth can attract return migrants and foreign workers.

Measure:
- work permits;
- return migration;
- remittances;
- housing demand;
- regional origin.

Migration affects both capacity and service demand.

## E30. Social protection

A resource boom can finance transfers, but permanent entitlements should be stress-tested under lower petroleum revenue.

Model:
- beneficiary count;
- unit benefit;
- inflation indexation;
- administrative cost;
- funding source;
- cost under low-price scenario.

## E31. Community investment by operators

Corporate social investment should be reported separately from statutory payments and public revenue.

Track:
- donor;
- amount;
- beneficiary;
- project;
- outcome.

Private philanthropy should not be counted as government expenditure.

## E32. Social outcome scorecard without ranking politics

The paper can display indicators but should not convert them into an overall political score.

A dashboard can show:
- direction;
- observation year;
- confidence;
- distribution;
- source.

Readers retain the decision about policy interpretation.

## E33. Local-content audit sample

Select suppliers across:
- catering;
- transport;
- fabrication;
- engineering;
- environmental services;
- legal/accounting;
- aviation;
- marine;
- construction.

For each, calculate domestic value using invoice, payroll, import and ownership data.

## E34. New-producer design principles

A newcomer should:
- set realistic categories;
- verify ownership;
- prioritize capability;
- protect safety;
- measure value added;
- publish results;
- review targets.

Targets should adjust as the supplier base matures.

## E35. Annex conclusion

The social case for petroleum cannot be established from GDP, supplier spending or project announcements alone. It requires recent household evidence, verified local value and service outcomes.

Guyana's local-content law, supplier system, household-data gaps and village-level climate-finance programmes provide a useful research setting for separating inputs from outcomes.
