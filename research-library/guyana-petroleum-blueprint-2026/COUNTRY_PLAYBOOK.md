# THE NEW-PRODUCER HANDBOOK
## A staged institutional workbook for an emerging oil-producing country or island state
Independent companion annex to The Guyana Sequence | Ragunauth Ramsaroop | 27 September 2026

This handbook is a transferability instrument, not a claim that Guyana's history is a universal model. It offers defined questions, document templates and alternative institutional structures from published country cases. Sovereign authorities remain responsible for policy, legislation, fiscal choices, treaty obligations and public consultation. Statements of Guyana's actual history refer to source IDs in SOURCE_REGISTER.md.

## 1. Start with a resource and institutional map rather than a model law

The first analytical task for a prospective petroleum state is to describe the country before petroleum income changes its financial structure. Guyana's 2009 Low Carbon Development Strategy, earlier commodity economy and decades of offshore exploration predated the first major commercial discovery. The country did not begin building every relevant technical or financial capability only after oil arrived. An island without large tropical forests or comparable offshore acreage has a different starting set of options. [S01, S22]

An initial country profile records offshore geology and the uncertainty of commercial reserves, coastal population and infrastructure, current export composition, public debt, annual tax revenue, national disaster losses, fisheries and tourism dependence, existing environmental agencies, maritime boundaries, regulatory staff, procurement integrity processes, public finance reporting and access to international specialist advice. These are separate datasets. A positive resource discovery changes the geological column but does not erase pre-existing environmental or fiscal exposure. The profile should state the latest observation year for every variable. Missing observations are recorded as missing.

The profile also identifies the institutional decision-makers whose legal mandates may overlap. Typical actors include a parliament, a ministry responsible for resources, finance authorities, a revenue agency, an environmental regulator, a national audit institution, a central bank, a coast guard or maritime authority, regional government, Indigenous or customary-rights institutions, professional regulators and an independent judiciary. Actual institutions differ under civil-law, common-law, hybrid and constitutionally decentralized jurisdictions. The workbook records the current legal mandate rather than silently assigning a new one.

A useful opening deliverable is a two-page public evidence map. The first page identifies the legal source, date of enactment and commencement, administering authority and current implementing regulations. The second page identifies data held by each institution, reporting lag, legal publication restrictions, unresolved boundary disputes and any independent review process. If existing law does not authorize the proposed activity, the gap is visible before budgets or investment agreements assume implementation.

A separate social and ecological baseline sets the denominator against which later claims will be tested. A coastline with dense tourism and reef ecosystems has a different vulnerability profile from Guyana's low coastal plain and broad forested interior. Climate adaptation, coastal fishery economics, drinking-water resilience and household service levels require direct measurement. Petroleum is a potential revenue source, not a substitute for obtaining the baseline.

## 2. Exploration stage: six documentary systems

The exploration stage has no guaranteed development or receipts. Guyana's experience includes decades of surveys and unsuccessful wells before Liza. A new state considering costly petroleum-specific institutions should record whether they are scaled to known commercial activity or to uncertain potential. In a country with limited human resources, time-bound specialist contracts and regional sharing agreements are among the institutional forms to compare against a permanent bureaucracy. Neither arrangement removes the need for retained institutional memory or independent conflict-of-interest screening. [S01]

The first documentary system is geological. A searchable block map links the licensing unit, coordinates, bathymetry, historical well records, independent reserve interpretation and uncertainty classification. All petroleum-in-place, contingent-resource and commercially recoverable-reserve statements use explicit definitions. Geological prospects are not parliamentary assets until commerciality is established and sales cash materializes.

The second system is fiscal. Each licence award or proposed agreement has a versioned record of fiscal instruments, stabilization clauses, obligations carried by the contractor, state obligations, allowable costs, relinquishment, abandonment and disputed-area exposure. Where parties negotiate confidentially, the public register states the legal basis and duration of nondisclosure and identifies which post-award terms enter the public domain. Tender dates and the grounds for different procurement methods are traceable.

The third system is ecological. Before drilling, the regulator receives baseline observations of water quality, seabed habitats, fisheries seasons, migration patterns, coastal protected areas, maritime traffic and prevailing currents. For transboundary risks, the record identifies neighboring states and the existing international response agreements. Geological survey and drilling permit terms should not be conflated with consent for a commercial producing field.

The fourth system is technical independence. A national authority can appoint external petroleum engineers, transaction counsel, public accountants and environmental scientists, subject to published conflict controls, confidentiality protections and knowledge-transfer requirements. The record lists each adviser, scope, deliverable, price, tenure, supervision and training obligation. Independent review is not established solely by naming an international consultancy; the published decision file needs evidence of the questions advisers examined and competing options considered.

The fifth system is public participation and rights. A dated register describes legally relevant consultations with fisher groups, coastal settlements, Indigenous and customary-rights holders, tourism representatives and other affected communities. Participation evidence records who received notice, technical materials and translation support, the matters raised and the regulator's reasoned response. National constitutional and treaty obligations determine how consultation, consent, compensation and review operate. The register should not expose personal data or confidential cultural sites.

The sixth system is contingency. A prospective exploration state maps the institutional owner of offshore emergency notification, regional deployment, oil-spill equipment, public-health coordination, aviation and maritime rescue, and access to claims assistance. At this stage a tabletop exercise may demonstrate gaps more clearly than a general emergency-policy statement. Its report identifies response time, jurisdiction and unresolved procurement, not a presumed ability to contain an unmodelled release.

These six systems can be prepared without predicting that commercial oil will be discovered. In an unsuccessful exploration outcome, their data and procedures may still inform geological archives, marine science and public procurement. The financial case for building them at a particular scale remains country-specific.

## 3. After discovery: the contract and approval decision file

A major commercial discovery introduces a different information problem. The state needs independent evidence about the field's technical and financial viability, while the operator controls substantial reservoir and project-cost information. The period between Guyana's 2015 Liza discovery and December 2019 first oil illustrates why agreements, environmental decisions and fiscal arrangements should be dated separately. Guyana's first NRF law was enacted during this pre-production period. [S01, S02, S04]

The development decision file begins with a technical summary listing verified discovered resources, development phases, forecast decline, required subsea facilities, FPSO or platform capacity, export route, power requirements, drilling schedule and decommissioning concept. A high production forecast must remain labelled as an operator case unless independently confirmed. Where the geology is uncertain, the file records alternative development sequences and their effect on public revenue rather than presenting one central production curve as an inevitable trajectory.

The commercial file compares the actual investment risks allocated by each contract form under the assumptions prevailing when signed. Production-sharing agreements, royalties and taxation, conventional licences and concession-tax systems, service contracts and state equity offer different combinations of upfront funding, price exposure, cost audit, abandonment liability and public oversight. The workbook does not presume any one form is appropriate for a given jurisdiction. It requires internally consistent cash flows, investor-return assumptions and explicit responsibility for failure to reach commercial production.

The fiscal model maintains separate input ledgers for price, recoverable costs, operating costs, investment timing, production, depreciation where relevant, state equity, royalties, profit sharing, corporate taxation, bonuses and decommissioning. The output ledger distinguishes gross petroleum sales, contractor receipts, the state's profit oil, royalty payments, other taxes, NRF deposits, investment income and money later appropriated through the budget. Each ledger must reconcile on a dated basis.

Guyana's signed 2016 Stabroek agreement includes a 2% royalty, a 75% cost-recovery ceiling and equal sharing of profit oil. Its later model announced a 10% royalty, a 65% recovery ceiling and 10% corporate tax for different licensing circumstances. The older contract's cost bank and the later model should occupy separate scenarios in any comparison. A 2026 cash outcome is not enough to reconstruct the entire risk and bargaining position at the date of the first agreement. [S02, S14, S15]

The environmental approval file is linked to but institutionally distinct from the financial model. A field's commercial value does not establish that mitigation, marine response, compensation, monitoring or financial assurance are adequate. The file identifies each applicable environmental impact assessment, alternatives considered, legal right to inspect and halt operations, monitoring data ownership, baseline methods, spill modelling assumptions and required independent studies.

The public record should preserve dissenting technical views. A regulator's reasoned decision has different evidentiary status from an operator's response to comments, a government announcement or a court ruling. Where litigation is pending, a risk register distinguishes the order in force, the relief requested and legal contingencies still unresolved. Guyana's May 2026 appeal concerning environmental financial assurance demonstrates why final manuscripts must read authenticated judgments instead of relying on competing summaries. [S20]

## 4. The first-oil readiness rehearsal

First oil often arrives before the initial public development model has stabilized. Under a production-sharing arrangement, cost recovery, crude lifting schedules and sales settlement affect the precise timing of government cash. A national oil accounting system must therefore function during commissioning, not several years later when petroleum receipts already dominate fiscal reporting. Guyana's H1 2026 figures demonstrate the importance of this distinction: some payments received in the first half corresponded to cargoes lifted near the end of 2025. [S07]

The rehearsal opens with a fictitious cargo. The operator supplies a metered production record and government entitlement calculation. The designated fiscal authority checks the volume, crude quality adjustment, eligible costs and royalty; the sales entity checks invoicing and settlement. The central bank confirms receipt. The finance ministry reconciles the money to the NRF statement. The parliamentary record then tracks any authorized transfer from the fund to the consolidated budget. Each step carries a date, accountable official, audit trail and route for a discrepancy.

No example needs to use confidential, live commercial numbers. A deliberately fictional first exercise can contain a missing invoice, late shipment, wrong exchange rate, duplicated cost invoice and incorrect posting of investment income as oil sales. The purpose is to test whether the participating institutions identify and correct differences without an opaque manual adjustment. Material issues found in exercises should appear in a remediation log with a revised protocol and retest date.

At the same time, a parallel environmental drill begins with a hypothetical subsea discharge in poor visibility and a second simulated warning from a neighboring maritime authority. The exercise records the emergency command structure, notification triggers, helicopter and vessel mobilization, sampling, shoreline protection priorities, public communication, legal evidence preservation and claims intake. A state with limited equipment may document formal agreements for shared regional response. The record should distinguish an agreement on paper from a tested mobilization time.

A third rehearsal concerns access to information. Citizens, parliamentarians and researchers try to trace a fictional cargo from the project's regulatory record to the public revenue ledger and verify an appropriation against the published budget. If essential data are lawfully confidential, the portal should specify why and what aggregated fields remain public. A portal with attractive graphics but irreconcilable periods or undefined terms is not a substitute for usable records.

The rehearsals create three different types of evidence: account reconciliation, operating-response capability and public accessibility. None is a measure of the ultimate success of an oil development, but each identifies specific institutional functions before first-year petroleum revenue becomes an ordinary budget assumption.

## 5. Designing a fund: alternatives rather than a copied withdrawal percentage

Guyana established a statutory Natural Resource Fund before first oil, replaced the first law in 2021 and changed withdrawal provisions in 2024. Ghana's 2011 revenue framework has dedicated stabilization and heritage components. Timor-Leste's Petroleum Fund dates to 2005 with a 2011 amendment. Norway has a mature global fund with a long-run real-return-linked fiscal guideline. The relevant analytical insight is the range of statutory and fiscal architectures, not a ranking of countries or a single numerical withdrawal formula. [S04, S24, S25, S26]

A country examining a resource fund first documents the problem the fund addresses. Annual petroleum cash is exposed to price and production changes. Underground reserves cannot be valued as risk-free cash. Fiscal law often needs a legal method for receiving proceeds, reporting investment performance, transferring a defined amount to the budget and exposing disbursements to ordinary procurement and audit rules. Different development needs, debt service, age structure, exchange-rate arrangements and climate liabilities alter the budget and savings trade-offs.

One analytical model separates stabilization assets from a long-term savings portfolio, with a published transfer rule for each. A second holds a single sovereign portfolio and ties normal-year spending to an estimate of sustainable returns. A third adopts a formula linked to a defined share of verified revenue or assets within constitutional budget procedures. A fourth hybrid model retains a legal spending envelope but adds a multi-year expenditure ceiling, emergency exception and a clear independent disclosure process. Each model creates different accounting, forecasting and governance demands.

The workbook requires a country-specific estimate of the usable cash buffer under a joint commodity-price and natural-disaster stress event. It identifies the unit in which spending obligations arise, the currency and liquidity of fund investments, the risk of simultaneous domestic recession, and a credible re-entry path after an emergency exception. If a rule permits extra withdrawals during a disaster, the document states who declares the exception, the amount and duration, oversight route and subsequent repayment or recalibration mechanism where applicable.

The core fund reconciliation is ordinary accounting. Opening cash, new petroleum deposits, investment return, other permitted adjustments and withdrawals together explain closing cash. Any transfer into the budget must reconcile to both the fund's statement and the government's budget execution statement. If royalties appear in oil-sale proceeds, they should not be added again under a different financial label. Cash received for prior-year liftings needs a visible timing bridge. One annual press release is not enough to replace account-level controls.

External audits establish whether statements are fairly prepared under an identified accounting standard. They do not establish whether the government selected or delivered the most valuable public projects. That second question requires public investment appraisal, procurement oversight, engineering completion evidence and independent service-delivery evaluation. Guyana's large fund cash balance in June 2026 and the IMF mission's July comments about investment monitoring illustrate the need to keep the two questions separate. [S07, S08]

A useful funding-options dossier therefore contains side-by-side legislation, liquidity simulations, administrative costs, governance responsibilities, spending plans, unknowns and public consultation records. The sovereign authority chooses the design under its constitutional and fiscal system. Publication of alternative assumptions allows later researchers to reconstruct the decision.

## 6. The petroleum revenue transparency ledger

An extractive revenue website serves different audiences. Parliament needs statutory compliance and account reconciliations. A central bank needs cash management and portfolio controls. Tax authorities need expenditure verification and settlement records. Local communities need understandable information about revenues, distribution programmes and environmental incidents. Researchers need data definitions and historical versions. A public ledger therefore needs more than a current total.

The minimal petroleum portion lists the project and contract identifier, fiscal instrument, observation period, quantity and unit, price basis, lift date, government entitlement, invoice period, payment date, receiving account and audit status. Project-specific records with legally protected commercial information may be aggregated, but the scope and legal basis of aggregation should be documented. An entity-level register records beneficial-ownership disclosures and changes subject to applicable privacy and security law.

The fund portion lists opening assets, new deposits by category, investment interest or income, valuation adjustment, lawful budget transfers and ending cash. Each published table includes its accounting basis and revision history. The budget portion separately identifies appropriations, procurement contract values, actual spending, verified physical completion, maintenance allocations and beneficiary results. A reader should be able to follow cash without assuming every transfer immediately produces a completed asset.

Environmental transparency is part of the same programme but has a different standard of evidence. The public permit register identifies drilling and production authorizations, impact assessment dates, permit expiry, reportable incident definitions, measured emissions, significant enforcement decisions, available monitoring data and any material remediation obligations. The administrative record captures which regulator confirmed a fact and which claim rests on operator self-report.

Civil-society representation cannot be reduced to web access. The EITI Board's May 2026 decision recorded stakeholder concerns about civil-society selection and initiated a full validation with external participation. An implementation workbook therefore documents who selected representatives, applicable rules, whether affected groups had access to technical support, how dissenting submissions were considered and whether public records provide a traceable response. The 2026 process should be described according to its final verified status before publication. [S16, S17]

A release process protects analytical integrity. Each dataset includes version, publication date, source agency, applicable period, methodological changes and a clear correction notice. A monthly series that changes definitions halfway through the year should not appear as a continuous trend without reconciliation. Differences between production, exports, oil sales and deposits should be shown rather than obscured.

## 7. Local participation and the value-added question

Governments use several forms of local participation: supplier qualification, procurement preferences or reservations, mandatory reporting, technical training, joint ventures, domestic processing, public education and access to finance. The domestic economic outcome depends on the mix of actual productive inputs rather than one gross procurement figure. Guyana's 2021 Local Content Act and its 2026 procurement reporting offer a case for examining the difference between an administratively eligible supplier and independently measured domestic value added. [S05, S07]

The workbook separates business registration, beneficial ownership, share of labour paid locally, imports embedded in purchased goods, plant and equipment owned within the jurisdiction, local subcontracting, professional accreditation, technology transfer and taxes retained. A service supplier may be a meaningful domestic employer even with imported equipment. Another company may book large contract value through a local subsidiary with most technical work completed abroad. Without disaggregation, both transactions look similar in gross purchase statistics.

A registry should permit independently checkable ownership and skills claims while respecting commercial confidentiality and individual privacy. Independent verification might compare procurement statements with audited supplier accounts, customs classifications, professional training completion and aggregate labour statistics. Alternative explanations for spending increases, including domestic construction inflation or reclassification of supplier categories, belong in the same analytical report.

Geography is equally important. Offshore supply chains often concentrate near ports and the capital, while inland and remote districts face different infrastructure needs. A national local-content percentage says little about benefits to hinterland communities. A map of procurement distribution, pay and completed infrastructure is therefore more informative than a single national total. For a small island, limited land, port throughput, environmental carrying capacity and seasonal tourism affect local supply opportunities.

The analysis must also measure possible displacement. Large operator contracts may raise wage rates and absorb engineers, drivers and electricians previously employed by clinics, schools, municipal agencies and non-oil firms. The relevant question is whether new training and productive capacity expand the overall skilled workforce faster than demand, not merely whether the petroleum industry hires more people. Labour-force and wage data require source, sample period and inflation adjustment.

## 8. Indigenous peoples, coastal livelihoods and public participation

An ethical and analytical case study makes participation visible as a process and an outcome. Guyana's forest-carbon policy commits part of eligible proceeds to Indigenous village-led development plans. Offshore petroleum activities primarily expose marine and coastal interests, while national spending decisions affect interior communities. Both require independently documented benefits and grievances. A small island's customary marine tenure, coastal fishery governance and tourism land rights may present different institutions and legal obligations. [S22, S23]

The country profile maps statutory land tenure, customary resource rights, relevant constitutional provisions, applicable international obligations, agreed consultation procedures and environmental justice claims. The appropriate consultation and consent process depends on those instruments and the circumstances of the project. Evidence includes dates, disclosure formats, translations, support for independent technical review and a record of the response to documented objections. A signed attendance sheet alone does not establish informed participation.

Community-finance claims require four distinct records: funds legally designated, sums transferred, locally selected projects and independently observed outcomes. A documented transfer to a village account is one fact. Community consent to a project, completion of the project, continuing operation and resulting improvements are additional facts requiring their own evidence. The full white paper should preserve contrary testimony and avoid extrapolating from a handful of cases to every participating village.

Maritime impacts require similar attention. Fishers' exposure depends on where vessels operate, exclusion zones, changes in landings, port congestion, prices, compensation procedures and baseline variability. An increase in national GDP does not prove unaffected fishers benefited. A properly specified marine impact study accounts for weather, seasonal migration, fisheries management, other industries and changes in reporting. The state and operator's ecological claims need independent verification where data are available.

The publication protocol should provide a right of factual response for materially criticized institutions and stakeholder organizations without allowing any source to veto findings. It should protect respondents who face credible confidentiality or retaliation risks. A clear chronology of complaints, agency responses and court decisions is more useful than a general assertion of agreement.

## 9. Environmental assurance across the full project lifecycle

Environmental permitting and oil-spill financial assurance are related but distinct. Licensing decides whether and under what conditions exploration or production is lawful. Financial assurance addresses availability and enforceability of resources after a covered event. Emergency response identifies assets and decisions available within the first hours and days. Ecological restoration addresses losses long after the emergency ends. Confusing these categories can create false confidence or understate actual liabilities.

Guyana's 2025 oil-pollution legislation and May 2026 appeal over permit-linked financial assurances offer detailed subjects for legal verification. The 2026 mid-year report described implementing regulations as under development and commencement as a future event. At a new publication cut-off, enacted legislation, operational regulations, permit guarantees and appellate orders must each be verified separately. This handbook does not assume any quoted insurance total without the actual contractual documents. [S07, S19, S20]

A project-specific risk register records the hazard, expected pathways, sensitive receptors, probability model and its uncertainty, monitoring threshold, legal reporting trigger, escalation owner, containment strategy, finance instrument and external response partner. Where the country lacks credible dispersion modelling or ocean-current data, the register states the limitation instead of displaying unwarranted decimal precision. Regional assistance agreements require contact details, transit permissions, operational compatibility and periodic exercises.

Insurance due diligence examines covered activities, limit per occurrence and aggregate, exclusions, deductible, insurer creditworthiness, jurisdiction for claims, financial assurance beneficiary, rapid-access liquidity, environmental restoration scope, third-party claims and decommissioning. A parent-company guarantee raises separate questions about capacity, jurisdiction, enforceability and its interaction with an operator's continuing liability. Evidence of a guarantee's existence is different from evidence that public liabilities are fully secured.

Monitoring covers normal operations as well as exceptional spills. Discharge records, produced-water treatment, routine and non-routine flaring, methane measurement, drilling waste, ballast handling, vessel collisions, protected species, beach contamination, accidental chemical release and supply-base impacts need project-specific methods and reporting thresholds. Annual mean conditions should not conceal short high-intensity incidents.

A small-island adaptation module places reef health, mangroves, beaches, freshwater lenses, tourism seasonality and evacuation logistics into the response plan. Guyana's forest MRV and coastal flood adaptation experience contain relevant monitoring methods, but the appropriate ecological baseline differs. A transferability assessment states precisely which laboratory methods, satellite products, emergency drills or public disclosure practices travel between jurisdictions and which require new expertise.

## 10. Public investment and durable service outcomes

Converting exhaustible revenue into durable national assets is a broader question than saving revenue in an investment fund. Infrastructure and human capital have different delivery schedules and lifecycle liabilities. Large petroleum receipts create an opportunity to finance capital projects but also expose a small economy to construction cost inflation, workforce shortages, poor maintenance planning and irreversible land-use changes. IMF staff's July 2026 mission highlighted Guyana's need for sound public investment appraisal and monitoring. [S08]

The public investment record begins with a published definition of the service problem, baseline conditions, alternatives, demand assumptions, environmental costs, construction schedule and lifecycle operation. For a hospital, the completion test includes staffing, power, maintenance, service hours and patient outcomes, not merely the ceremonial opening. For roads, the test covers load conditions, all-weather access, drainage and maintenance. For coastal barriers, the test covers projected flood risk, residual exposure and local ecological impacts.

The procurement register connects each project to tender design, qualified bidders where publicly available, evaluation criteria, contract award, change orders, schedule extensions, independent inspection and defects liability. It distinguishes price savings during tender from total cost after scope changes. A public project portfolio report should identify projects whose maintenance liabilities exceed planned operating budgets before those assets enter service.

Fiscal analysis compares infrastructure delivery capacity with projected annual petroleum cash under multiple prices and production paths. A legally available withdrawal is not evidence that domestic construction supply can absorb the resulting spending without delays or price escalation. A separate model estimates import demand, skilled labour, land requirements and potential crowding of non-oil investment. Uncertainty in domestic absorption is reported alongside uncertainty in the oil price.

Social outcomes need disaggregation. Regional living standards, access to water and electricity, education completion, maternal and child health, housing affordability, travel time and employment among women and younger workers are plausible indicators. Their applicability and target levels follow national development plans and community consultation. Surveys taken years before first oil should remain marked historical rather than be presented as current evidence.

## 11. Five-year observation schedule

The workbook groups observation by operational stage rather than guaranteeing a calendar date for oil production. Year 1 begins when a jurisdiction formally launches a resource-governance work programme, whether or not a commercial discovery exists. Every item below is a record requested for research and possible administrative review, not a mandatory legal prescription.

During months 0-3 the record covers petroleum licence and geological holdings, current law and commencement status, fiscal exposures, marine baseline, existing disaster protocols and the capacity of existing agencies. The finance ministry assembles a preliminary risk and revenue-uncertainty register. A ministry or regulator charged with petroleum maintains the licensing and contract inventory. Statutory audit bodies map their access to records. Representative stakeholder institutions identify consultation requirements under applicable law.

During months 4-6 the record includes independent technical advice procured, review of existing disclosure obligations, available fiscal and contract design alternatives, cost-audit capacity, ecological knowledge gaps and an initial incident communications exercise. Where petroleum prospects are not commercially proven, the budget scenario uses zero commercial oil revenue as its central binding reality rather than projected receipts. If a commercial discovery has occurred, engineering and fiscal scenarios identify range and timing.

During months 7-12 the country dossier details chosen regulatory and contract procedures, EIA scoping, transboundary notifications where required, future fund legislation options, sample reconciliation records, independent audit mandates and a funded skills plan. Publication may be staged in line with lawful data protection, but the record explains disclosure omissions. A tabletop exercise tests resource and environmental decisions across agencies with no assumptions of operational perfection.

During months 13-24 the observed documents reflect the actual development stage: environmental permit record, drilling or commissioning inspections, contract-cost audits, any effective resource fund legislation, validated production metering where applicable and reporting systems. A jurisdiction still in exploration does not need fictitious profit-oil tables; it records progress against exploration-stage tasks instead. Any delay in production updates the fiscal scenario and budget expectations.

During years 3-5 the portfolio review examines signed versus actual development schedules, year-by-year revenue reconciliations if production has begun, actual fund deposits and transfers, fiscal stress-test assumptions, public investment delivery, domestic-value measurements, environmental incidents, administrative appeal outcomes and independent community outcome evidence. Comparable historical series require a fixed base and disclose methodological revisions.

At each stage the workbook reserves a correction section. A delayed field, adverse environmental judgment, commodity shock or public procurement failure changes facts, which changes the scenario. Reviewers should be able to reconstruct the original assumptions and the date those assumptions were revised. The workbook does not erase an earlier forecast when a later publication supersedes it.

## 12. Ten cross-agency exercises for the implementation team

Exercise one asks the petroleum ministry and environmental regulator to trace a fictional exploration block from acreage designation through EIA scope and final approval. Record the public legal documents and any unresolved overlap of powers.

Exercise two asks independent technical counsel to quantify how a dry-well outcome changes the proposed administrative budget. Document the difference between spending on general-purpose geological data and spending that depends on an operating hydrocarbon industry.

Exercise three uses the executed PSA or concession terms to trace a fictional field's sales, allowable cost, royalty, taxation and state receipt. The revenue authority records how disputes and payment delays affect cash timing.

Exercise four asks the central bank, finance ministry and auditor to reconcile the same fictional oil payment across accounts, including a settlement posted one month after physical lifting. A mismatch creates a correction record, not a hidden balancing item.

Exercise five asks the parliamentary budget office to distinguish legally available fund transfers from cash already collected, projected year-end receipts and operational spending commitments. Run an illustrative low-price year and document different buffer designs without prescribing a single fiscal rule.

Exercise six asks the environmental regulator and coast guard to test a simulated spill with a neighboring state affected. Record who authorizes sampling, emergency procurement, shoreline access and claims intake. Repeat the same exercise under a power outage or severe coastal storm.

Exercise seven asks the procurement authority to evaluate a large petroleum-financed infrastructure project before and after a hypothetical 25% construction-cost overrun. Include maintenance, skill shortages and the consequences of delaying other projects.

Exercise eight asks the local supplier registry and an independent economist to reconcile a high gross procurement announcement with wage payments, imported equipment, local ownership, subcontracting and retained domestic profits.

Exercise nine asks a protected independent panel to document stakeholder participation in a fictional coastal development approval. The test is whether different views reach the decision-maker and receive reasoned responses, not whether every party agrees.

Exercise ten asks a mixed technical review team to assess a hypothetical halt in offshore output coinciding with flooding and a sharp oil-price fall. Recalculate cash, essential services, contract obligations, ecological emergency funding and the credibility of public communications.

Every exercise produces a date, participating institution, source documents, observations, corrective actions, verification method and retest date. An uncompleted exercise is shown as outstanding rather than assigned a favorable qualitative label.

## 13. What Guyana's case does not establish

Guyana's early NRF legislation is a historical observation. It does not by itself prove that a particular savings rate, withdrawal formula or investment portfolio has maximized intergenerational welfare. Such a claim would need long-run counterfactual analysis, accounting and household outcomes. A large fund balance and rapid production growth are not interchangeable with measured school performance, flood resilience or reductions in poverty. [S04, S07, S10]

The 2016 and later model PSAs document different fiscal terms in different commercial circumstances. These documents do not establish that the same contract works for a shallow-water field, a field with state equity, a landlocked onshore producer or an island whose public balance sheet cannot bear a large contingent spill liability. The exact risk allocation and counterparty financing capacity must be reconstructed for each case. [S02, S14]

Guyana's forest programme offers a separate climate-finance case, but a country without forests of comparable scale has no identical carbon asset. A country's petroleum revenues and forest-credit receipts have different legal rights, accounting bases and environmental effects. Carbon-credit eligibility and contract value do not establish measured nationwide carbon neutrality or a permanent right to petroleum expansion. [S22, S23]

The existence of enacted pollution legislation is not synonymous with commencement, funded enforcement or tested emergency operations. An appeal about financial assurance does not settle every possible ecological-loss scenario. The research must retain authenticated legal sources and independent insurance analysis as open requirements. [S19, S20]

Finally, country comparisons have different production histories and social baselines. Ghana's revenue law, Norway's mature financial fund, Timor-Leste's sovereign petroleum fund and Suriname's pre-first-oil preparations are documentary comparators rather than a league table. A useful successor study would collect consistent country-year data, label changes in accounting methodology, document the distinct welfare measures available and subject any causal claims to a defensible identification strategy. [S24-S27]

## 14. Compact reusable templates

**Country opening record:** Name of jurisdiction; programme initiation date; discovery status; resource classification; public debt observation year; existing royalty and tax base; existing environmental and marine protections; sensitive ecosystems; customary-rights framework; coastal and inland settlements; responsible institutions; independent audit mandate; unresolved treaty and maritime questions; last household-survey observation year; central-bank data accessibility.

**Agreement comparison record:** Contract identifier and version; signature and effective dates; contract model; legal approval; resource and price uncertainty at the time; development and abandonment obligations; royalty, tax and profit-sharing terms; cost eligibility and audit rights; stabilization and dispute resolution; assignment and beneficial ownership; environmental and financial assurance; public disclosure status; independent counsel and conflict review.

**Fund reconciliation record:** Fiscal period; audit status; opening cash and investment portfolio value; petroleum deposits by source; investment income and valuation adjustments; authorized withdrawal; receiving treasury account; budget-law reference; closing cash; reconciliation difference; publication and revision dates; public audit opinion.

**Environmental assurance record:** Project and licence identifier; baseline surveys; EIA date; permit conditions; discharge and emissions thresholds; incident reports; independent monitoring; response assets and mobilization results; insurance contract and exclusions verified; parent or other guarantee where applicable; claim jurisdiction; decommissioning security; statutory effective dates; public grievance and judicial review procedures.

**Community outcomes record:** Affected stakeholder class; legal consultation rule; disclosure and meeting dates; dissenting and supporting submissions; reasoned agency response; monetary entitlement and transfer; locally approved projects; construction and operation verification; measured benefit and distribution; continuing grievances; independent reviewer.

**Public investment outcome record:** Project identifier; approved business case; procurement method; financing mix; cost baseline; contract award and variations; completion evidence; maintenance provision; climate or environmental exposure; service baseline; actual beneficiaries; operation performance; auditor's findings; outstanding remedial work.

**Correction and disclosure record:** Dataset or legal assertion corrected; original publication date; conflicting source; confidence and reason for correction; old and new values; whether previous analytical conclusions change; approving researcher; next source verification date; cross-referenced exhibits.

The final 140-160 substantive-page monograph will integrate these templates with year-by-year datasets, a verified comparison of fund rules, legal text citations, multi-scenario modelling and independent external technical review. Until then, this playbook is an openly labelled research instrument attached to the working edition.
