# Research objective

Part II reconstructs the commercial and legal architecture governing the Stabroek Block without reducing the agreement to a single royalty rate or a single government-take figure.

The final analysis must distinguish:

- information available when the agreement was signed;
- geological and commercial risk at the time;
- signed fiscal terms;
- the mechanics of cost recovery;
- actual project costs submitted by the contractor;
- government audit findings;
- the status of disputed adjustments;
- later model fiscal terms developed under different geological conditions;
- later licences and agreements;
- petroleum revenues actually received.

No overall political score or contract ranking will be assigned.

# Chapter 5 research dossier
## Liza 2015 and the information shock

The Liza-1 well is recorded by the Petroleum Management Programme as an exploration oil well dated 15 May 2015. [S36]

The final discovery analysis should establish a chronology of information becoming available to both state and contractor:

1. pre-drill geological interpretation;
2. well spud and drilling;
3. hydrocarbon indications;
4. public discovery announcement;
5. appraisal drilling;
6. early resource estimates;
7. commerciality assessment;
8. concept selection;
9. development planning;
10. production-licence approval.

This chronology matters because a negotiating position changes as geological uncertainty declines.

The paper should not infer that information known after appraisal was available to negotiators at an earlier date. The correct counterfactual question is: what would a technically informed government and investor have known at each decision point?

Required evidence:

- original Liza discovery announcements;
- appraisal well records;
- resource-estimate revisions by date;
- oil-price environment during 2015-2016;
- deepwater development cost estimates available at the time;
- comparable frontier-basin fiscal terms;
- financing and execution risk.

## 6.2 Headline fiscal mechanics

Existing official and audit sources establish the need to model the 75% cost-recovery ceiling as a ceiling, not as an assertion that actual economic costs always equal 75% of revenue.

The flagship should show at least four numerical states:

A. recoverable costs exceed the monthly ceiling;
B. recoverable costs are below the ceiling;
C. production exceeds sales;
D. later project costs replenish the cost bank.

The simplified waterfall is pedagogical only. The final fiscal model must trace actual contract accounting.

## 6.3 Effective date and recoverability

The government-published 2018-2020 audit material records an exception involving insurance costs incurred before 7 October 2016 and states that, subject to specified pre-contract-cost treatment, costs before the effective date were not recoverable. [S42]

This is important because it demonstrates why the contract chapter must distinguish:

- signing date;
- effective date;
- pre-contract costs expressly recognized;
- costs incurred after effective date;
- costs later challenged by auditors.

The final manuscript should not infer final financial recovery from an audit exception. It must identify whether the contractor agreed, whether a credit was processed, whether government accepted the resolution and whether any dispute remained.

# Chapter 7 research dossier
## Cost recovery, accounting and the cost bank

Cost recovery is where fiscal theory becomes accounting practice.

The 2018-2020 audit published through the Petroleum Management Programme reviews cost and revenue components and includes detailed exceptions. [S42]

The final chapter should group audit issues by category rather than listing them randomly.

## 7.1 Proposed audit taxonomy

### Allocation across blocks
Costs that benefit multiple licences or operations require a defensible allocation basis.

### Timing
Costs incurred before or after the effective date, capitalized costs and costs recognized in later periods require correct treatment.

### Duplicate or erroneous charges
Duplicate invoices, coding mistakes and costs assigned to the wrong block should be isolated.

### General and administrative costs
Office, overhead and shared-services allocation should follow contractual accounting rules.

### Emergency and environmental costs
Studies and response systems may benefit more than one project or licence and should be allocated consistently with the agreement.

### Valuation
Cost oil and profit oil depend on volume and price rules. Valuation methods must be audited separately from cost classification.

### Affiliate transactions
Related-party charges require particular attention to arm's-length principles and contract rules.

### Decommissioning
Future abandonment and restoration obligations require explicit treatment.

## 7.2 The cost bank

A transparent cost-bank model should include:

Opening unrecovered costs  
+ new recoverable costs  
+ permitted adjustments  
- cost oil recovered  
= closing unrecovered costs.

The model needs project and category tags.

A single block-wide number can hide the effect of new project approvals. If development spending rises while older costs fall, government profit-oil entitlement can change materially without any amendment to the contract.

## 7.3 Audit status categories

Every audit item in the paper should carry one of these labels:

- identified;
- contractor response received;
- government response recorded;
- credit accepted;
- credit posted;
- dispute unresolved;
- arbitration;
- final settlement;
- no public evidence of disposition.

This prevents a proposed adjustment from being reported as money already recovered.

# Chapter 8 research dossier
## Later model terms and changing information

Later fiscal terms were developed after commercial success had reduced frontier uncertainty.

The flagship should compare agreement generations without implying that later terms were available under identical conditions in 2016.

Comparison fields should include:

- royalty;
- cost-recovery ceiling;
- profit sharing;
- corporate tax;
- signing bonus;
- acreage size;
- relinquishment;
- ring-fencing;
- local-content obligations;
- environmental obligations;
- decommissioning;
- audit rights;
- beneficial ownership;
- dispute settlement.

The core research principle is temporal fairness. Contract terms negotiated after multiple commercial discoveries should not be used as if they were contemporaneous market evidence for an earlier frontier negotiation.

At the same time, the historical agreement should be assessed against comparable agreements and available expertise at the time, not insulated from scrutiny by uncertainty.

Both propositions can be true.

# Chapter 9 research dossier
## Contract governance for a newcomer

A transferable contract-governance system should focus on process quality rather than copying Guyana's percentages.

## Before negotiation

- establish a multidisciplinary negotiating team;
- preserve state control over geological data;
- obtain independent reservoir and petroleum-economics advice;
- model investor returns under several price and discovery outcomes;
- map fiscal interactions with tax law;
- identify environmental and decommissioning liabilities;
- publish conflict-of-interest rules;
- define confidentiality boundaries.

## During negotiation

Maintain a decision log showing:

- clause proposed;
- government's objective;
- contractor position;
- independent technical assessment;
- fiscal effect;
- legal effect;
- environmental effect;
- final negotiated result;
- approval authority.

## Before signature

Complete:

- fiscal model review;
- legal consistency review;
- environmental-law review;
- tax review;
- audit-rights review;
- beneficial-ownership check;
- dispute-resolution analysis;
- decommissioning-liability review;
- parliamentary/public disclosure assessment.

## After signature

Create a contract administration calendar:

- work commitments;
- relinquishment dates;
- development submissions;
- production licences;
- annual budgets;
- cost statements;
- audit windows;
- royalty due dates;
- tax filings;
- environmental reporting;
- training obligations;
- decommissioning provisions.

# Initial documentary findings from government-published audit material

The 2018-2020 audit provides useful evidence about the operation of the agreement in practice.

It includes exceptions relating to:

- costs coded to the wrong block;
- allocation of office-related construction;
- allocation of emergency-response study costs;
- insurance costs before the effective date;
- duplicate charges.

The paper should use these examples to demonstrate the role of audit rights rather than to generalize from individual exceptions.

The audit also discusses government profit-oil validation and cost-oil valuation. This gives the flagship a basis for explaining how volume, valuation and cost accounting interact.

A key publication control follows:

Audit findings are evidence of an audit process. They are not, by themselves, evidence that every exception was finally resolved or collected.
