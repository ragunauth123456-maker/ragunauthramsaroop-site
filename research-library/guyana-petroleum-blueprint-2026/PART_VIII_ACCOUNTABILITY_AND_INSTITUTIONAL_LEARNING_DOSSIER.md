# PART VIII RESEARCH DOSSIER
## Transparency, Cost Audits, Procurement, Parliamentary/Judicial Oversight and Public Data

Working research dossier for Chapters 39-43 of the 520-560-page flagship publication.  
Evidence cut-off: 27 September 2026.  
Status: research stage. Legal and public-finance review pending.

# Research objective

A resource-governance system is not demonstrated by the number of reports published.

The flagship will test five linked questions:

1. Are material contracts, licences, production data and public receipts disclosed?
2. Are figures timely enough to support scrutiny?
3. Is independent challenge institutionally possible?
4. Are audit findings resolved and traceable?
5. Are public-finance and social outcomes visible after money leaves the petroleum account?

Transparency, oversight and enforcement will therefore appear as separate categories.

# Chapter 39 research dossier
## EITI and extractive disclosure

Guyana joined EITI in 2017. The EITI International Secretariat records an earlier 2022 Validation under the 2019 Standard and a new full Validation beginning 15 May 2026. [S70]

The EITI Board's 12 May 2026 decision records concern about continuing lack of full stakeholder confidence regarding the independence of the selection of civil-society representatives to the multi-stakeholder group. The Board chose to proceed with a full Validation and an external expert. [S69]

This is a formally attributed EITI concern.

The final flagship should not convert it into a broad judgment about political legitimacy.

## Timeliness

In a rapidly growing petroleum industry, a report covering fiscal year N but published in N+2 has different decision value from quarterly public data.

The paper should show disclosure lag explicitly.

# Chapter 40 research dossier
## Cost audits and revenue assurance

Cost-recovery audit is one of the clearest examples of why publication is not the same as final resolution.

The government-published IHS Markit final audit for 1999-2017 says the contractor submitted cumulative costs of about USD1.678 billion for review and that the audit team identified grounds to dispute about USD214.4 million plus overhead adjustments. [S71]

A later Ministry update says the government dispute was moved toward the agreement's sole-expert procedure and describes multiple audit workstreams. [S72]

These are distinct stages.

The final audit tracker should distinguish:
- amount submitted;
- amount audited;
- audit exception;
- government claim/dispute;
- contractor response;
- negotiation;
- sole expert;
- arbitration;
- final accepted adjustment;
- cost-bank credit;
- cash/revenue effect.

No audit exception will be reported as recovered revenue before the final accounting event is evidenced.

## Revenue assurance institutions

The chapter should map:
- Ministry of Natural Resources;
- Guyana Revenue Authority;
- Ministry of Finance;
- Bank of Guyana;
- external petroleum auditors;
- Attorney General/legal teams;
- independent experts.

For each:
- mandate;
- staffing;
- data access;
- audit window;
- dispute authority;
- publication obligation.

## Audit-cycle metric

Measure:
audit period end  
→ audit engagement  
→ fieldwork  
→ draft report  
→ contractor response  
→ final report  
→ dispute  
→ resolution.

A system with strong audit rights but prolonged unresolved findings has a different performance profile from a system with rapid final adjustments.

The public investment chapter must therefore connect:

- variation;
- physical completion;
- payment;

The IMF 2026 mission statement encouraged continued strengthening of procurement practices and consistent compliance as public spending expands. [S64]

# Accountability architecture

The flagship should present accountability as six layers.

## Layer 1. Rule
Law, contract, permit or regulation defines an obligation.

## Layer 2. Measurement
A competent body measures performance.

## Layer 3. Disclosure
The evidence is published in usable form.

## Layer 4. Independent challenge
Auditor, court, parliament, civil society, affected community or expert can contest the record.

## Layer 5. Correction
A substantiated problem leads to adjustment, sanction, remediation or rule change.

## Layer 6. Outcome
The correction affects revenue, environmental performance, service delivery or public confidence.

An institution that performs strongly at one layer might perform differently at another.

# Publication boundary

Part VIII will not treat disclosure as proof of performance.

It will not treat an audit finding as proof of recovered revenue.

It will not treat a contested EITI process as an overall judgment on a government or opposition.

It will document institutions, dates, evidence, disagreements and observable corrective actions.
