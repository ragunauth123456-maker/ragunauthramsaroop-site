# TECHNICAL ANNEX L
## Integrated national petroleum risk register

Purpose: show how fiscal, operational, environmental, social and institutional risks interact. The register describes controls without predicting political or market outcomes.

## L1. Risk fields

Every risk record should contain:
- ID;
- category;
- event;
- cause;
- affected asset;
- evidence;
- existing control;
- control owner;
- early indicator;
- contingency;
- review date;
- residual uncertainty.

Avoid a single political score.

## L2. Geological risk

Event: discovery is smaller or less productive than expected.
Control:
- independent resource review;
- staged appraisal;
- conservative public forecasts.

Budget response:
Do not create permanent spending before commercial certainty.

## L3. Oil-price risk

Event: sustained lower realized price.
Control:
- conservative budget assumptions;
- fund buffer;
- stress test;
- flexible capital programme.

## L4. Production risk

Event: reservoir or equipment underperformance.
Control:
- regulator access;
- maintenance;
- redundant systems;
- conservative forecast.

## L5. Project-delay risk

Event: first oil delayed.
Control:
- schedule ranges;
- no borrowing based solely on operator date;
- periodic forecast revision.

## L6. Cost-recovery risk

Event: cost bank higher than forecast.
Control:
- early audit;
- project tagging;
- related-party review;
- scenario model.

## L7. Transfer-pricing risk

Event: related-party costs exceed arm's-length value.
Control:
- documentation;
- comparables;
- audit;
- contract rights.

## L8. Revenue-collection risk

Event: invoice or settlement error.
Control:
- cargo ID;
- independent calculation;
- bank reconciliation;
- audit.

## L9. Sovereign-fund market risk

Event: financial asset loss.
Control:
- diversified allocation;
- benchmark;
- liquidity;
- risk limit.

## L10. Withdrawal risk

Event: withdrawals rise faster than durable revenue.
Control:
- statutory rule;
- parliament;
- long-run scenario;
- published calculation.

## L11. Public-investment inflation risk

Event: construction cost rises.
Control:
- sequencing;
- unit-cost database;
- independent estimates.

## L12. Maintenance risk

Event: new assets deteriorate.
Control:
- lifecycle budget;
- condition monitoring;
- responsible operator.

## L13. Debt risk

Event: debt service rises as oil revenue falls.
Control:
- debt strategy;
- maturity;
- currency limits;
- asset-liability analysis.

## L14. Public-enterprise risk

Event: SOE losses create fiscal transfer.
Control:
- audited statements;
- borrowing limits;
- performance contracts;
- guarantee register.

## L15. Spill risk

Event: major offshore release.
Control:
- prevention;
- capping;
- command;
- response assets;
- financial assurance;
- exercise.

## L16. Financial-assurance risk

Event: available security below actual response need.
Control:
- periodic severe-case estimate;
- insurance review;
- parent support;
- regulator revision.

## L17. Fisheries risk

Event: pollution or exclusion affects fishing.
Control:
- baseline;
- consultation;
- closure protocol;
- claims.

## L18. Biodiversity risk

Event: cumulative offshore activity harms sensitive species.
Control:
- monitoring;
- mitigation hierarchy;
- permit limits;
- independent review.

## L19. Climate-transition risk

Event: long-run oil demand or financing changes.
Control:
- lower-price scenarios;
- economic diversification;
- no reliance on indefinite high prices.

## L20. Carbon-market risk

Event: methodology or buyer eligibility changes.
Control:
- separate climate-finance scenarios;
- diversified financing;
- verified issuance.

## L21. Local-content inflation risk

Event: mandatory local procurement raises cost where capacity is insufficient.
Control:
- capacity study;
- phased targets;
- competition.

## L22. Front-company risk

Event: nominal local ownership hides foreign control.
Control:
- beneficial ownership;
- audit;
- enforcement.

## L23. Skills risk

Event: shortage delays projects and weakens regulator.
Control:
- workforce model;
- compensation;
- training;
- targeted recruitment.

## L24. Household inequality risk

Event: national income rises while lower-income real consumption lags.
Control:
- current survey;
- targeted services;
- regional data.

The paper describes evidence and policy tools without choosing an electoral programme.

## L25. Housing risk

Event: rent and land prices rise.
Control:
- planning;
- serviced land;
- data;
- infrastructure.

## L26. Food-price risk

Event: wage and demand shock raises food cost.
Control:
- market monitoring;
- agricultural productivity;
- logistics.

## L27. Procurement risk

Event: weak tender or contract management reduces value.
Control:
- competition;
- beneficial ownership;
- variation audit;
- outcome review.

## L28. Corruption risk

Event: public or private decision is distorted for improper benefit.
Control:
- conflict declarations;
- procurement transparency;
- audit;
- investigation;
- enforcement.

Risk description should not imply misconduct without evidence.

## L29. EITI participation risk

Event: stakeholder confidence in representation declines.
Control:
- transparent selection;
- documented consultation;
- independent process.

Guyana's ongoing 2026 validation provides a documented reason to include the risk. [S69-S70]

## L30. Statistical risk

Event: stale data distort policy.
Control:
- survey calendar;
- revision policy;
- metadata.

## L31. Cyber risk

Event: production or financial data compromised.
Control:
- access control;
- backup;
- incident response;
- offline archive.

## L32. Records risk

Event: contract or permit history lost.
Control:
- versioned digital archive;
- redundant storage;
- retention law.

## L33. Litigation risk

Event: permit or fiscal dispute creates uncertainty.
Control:
- reasoned decisions;
- legal review;
- complete administrative record.

## L34. Political-transition continuity risk

Event: institutional records or procedures change abruptly after an election or cabinet change.
Control:
- statutory roles;
- professional civil service;
- archived data;
- audited accounts.

This is an institutional continuity issue, not a forecast about any election.

## L35. Transboundary risk

Event: spill or maritime dispute affects neighbour.
Control:
- treaties;
- notification;
- mutual assistance;
- maritime clarity.

## L36. Decommissioning risk

Event: operator exits before full closure liability is funded.
Control:
- reserve;
- security;
- assignment conditions;
- periodic estimate.

## L37. Risk interaction

Risks combine.

Example:
price fall + project delay + construction inflation can reduce revenue while increasing project cost.

The integrated scenario should be tested annually.

## L38. Early-warning dashboard

Indicators:
- oil price;
- production variance;
- cost-bank variance;
- fund withdrawal ratio;
- construction inflation;
- debt service;
- regulator vacancy;
- audit backlog;
- permit breaches;
- grievances;
- household inflation.

## L39. Control assurance

Each control should have:
- design owner;
- operating owner;
- test;
- frequency;
- evidence;
- deficiency;
- remediation.

A policy document without an operating test is not assurance.

## L40. Risk review cycle

Quarterly:
financial and production risk.

Semi-annual:
project delivery, local content, labour.

Annual:
environment, sovereign fund, social data, institutional controls.

After material event:
special review.

## L41. Annex conclusion

A petroleum state needs one integrated risk picture because shocks cross institutional boundaries. The risk register supports disciplined preparation without turning uncertainty into a political judgment.
