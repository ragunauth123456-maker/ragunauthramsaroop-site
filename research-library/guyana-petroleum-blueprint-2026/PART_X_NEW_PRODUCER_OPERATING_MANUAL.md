# PART X
## New Producer Operating Manual

A staged implementation companion to the Guyana petroleum case study.  
Designed for emerging offshore producers, including island states.  
Evidence cut-off: 27 September 2026.

This manual translates research questions into administrative actions.

It does not prescribe a political choice, fiscal regime, spending ratio, state ownership model or energy policy.

The receiving country must adapt every action to its constitution, geology, marine ecology, debt, labour market, Indigenous/customary rights, climate exposure and international obligations.

# Stage 0
## Before a commercial discovery

### Objective

Build enough public capacity to govern exploration without creating permanent spending commitments against hypothetical oil revenue.

## 0.1 Legal map

Publish a one-page legal map identifying:
- ownership of petroleum;
- licensing authority;
- environmental authority;
- maritime authority;
- tax authority;
- customs;
- central bank;
- national audit institution;
- parliament;
- courts;
- disaster-response command.

For every institution record:
- statute;
- date in force;
- responsible office;
- power;
- reporting duty;
- appeal route.

## 0.2 Acreage and geological-data register

Maintain:
- block coordinates;
- licences;
- expiry;
- work commitments;
- relinquishment;
- operator;
- beneficial ownership;
- well records;
- seismic ownership;
- confidentiality expiry.

The state should preserve institutional access to geological data after individual consultants or ministers leave office.

## 0.3 Environmental baseline

Before intensive drilling, collect:
- currents;
- bathymetry;
- water quality;
- fisheries;
- sensitive habitats;
- protected areas;
- shoreline receptors;
- tourism assets;
- marine traffic.

For an island:
- reefs;
- beaches;
- desalination intakes;
- cruise terminals;
- hurricane exposure.

Record:
- collector;
- method;
- date;
- raw-data ownership;
- public access;
- uncertainty.

## 0.4 Fiscal options

Model at least:
- royalty/tax licence;
- production sharing;
- state equity where legally feasible;
- hybrid options.

For each:
- dry-hole case;
- marginal discovery;
- large discovery;
- low price;
- high cost;
- development delay;
- abandonment.

Do not choose a fiscal system from headline government-take percentages alone.

## 0.5 Expert capacity

Create a roster for:
- petroleum law;
- reservoir engineering;
- petroleum economics;
- tax;
- audit;
- marine science;
- oil-spill response;
- insurance;
- public finance.

Every external contract should include:
- conflict check;
- scope;
- deliverable;
- price;
- knowledge transfer;
- data ownership;
- handover.

## 0.6 Maritime boundaries

Identify:
- agreed boundaries;
- unresolved claims;
- arbitration/treaty options;
- disputed blocks;
- notification obligations.

Do not market disputed acreage as if title risk does not exist.

## Stage 0 gate

The country passes Stage 0 when it has:
- legal map;
- licence register;
- data policy;
- environmental baseline plan;
- fiscal-option model;
- independent adviser panel;
- conflict rules;
- boundary assessment;
- exploration emergency plan.

Commercial discovery is not required.

# Stage 1
## After a commercial discovery

### Objective

Reduce information asymmetry before committing the country to a development framework.

## 1.1 Independent resource review

Obtain:
- discovery data;
- appraisal results;
- fluid characteristics;
- reservoir uncertainty;
- development concepts;
- recoverable-resource range.

Separate:
- petroleum in place;
- contingent resources;
- reserves;
- commercial development case.

## 1.2 Negotiation decision file

For each material contract clause record:
- state objective;
- operator proposal;
- independent adviser view;
- fiscal effect;
- legal effect;
- environmental effect;
- final text;
- approving authority.

Preserve draft/version history under lawful confidentiality.

## 1.3 Fiscal model

Model:
- production;
- price;
- capital cost;
- operating cost;
- royalty;
- cost recovery;
- profit share;
- tax;
- state equity;
- bonuses;
- decommissioning.

Stress:
- low price;
- delay;
- cost overrun;
- lower recovery;
- outage;
- future project cost additions.

## 1.4 Environmental decision file

Require:
- terms of reference;
- alternatives;
- baseline;
- cumulative effects;
- spill scenarios;
- climate effects;
- public consultation;
- mitigation;
- monitoring;
- financial assurance.

A development decision should show how material public comments were treated.

## 1.5 Decommissioning

Before production:
- estimate abandonment cost;
- define security mechanism;
- set contribution timing;
- establish residual liability;
- define asset removal and site restoration;
- audit the fund/security.

## Stage 1 gate

Before final development approval:
- independent resource review complete;
- contract legally reviewed;
- fiscal model independently challenged;
- EIA complete;
- spill response tested;
- decommissioning plan funded;
- financial assurance documented;
- disclosure decision recorded.

# Stage 2
## First-oil readiness

### Objective

Prove the revenue, environmental and emergency systems before commercial cash arrives.

## 2.1 Meter and production chain

Test:
well  
→ FPSO processing  
→ storage  
→ cargo measurement  
→ lifting.

Independently verify calibration.

## 2.2 Sales and entitlement chain

Test:
cargo  
→ price  
→ royalty  
→ cost recovery  
→ profit share  
→ invoice  
→ settlement.

Use a fictional cargo rehearsal.

## 2.3 Sovereign revenue chain

Test:
receipt  
→ sovereign account  
→ public notice  
→ accounting  
→ audit.

If a sovereign fund exists, verify:
- legal account;
- custodian;
- signatories;
- investment rules;
- publication;
- audit.

## 2.4 Emergency drill

Test a serious hypothetical offshore event.

Measure:
- detection;
- notification;
- command activation;
- equipment mobilization;
- regional assistance;
- public communication;
- environmental sampling;
- claims intake.

Record actual exercise times.

## 2.5 Public transparency rehearsal

Ask an independent user to trace:
- licence;
- permit;
- production;
- sale;
- government receipt;
- fund deposit.

Missing definitions become a remediation item.

## Stage 2 gate

First-oil readiness is not complete until:
- meter verification;
- fiscal rehearsal;
- bank reconciliation;
- environmental monitoring;
- emergency exercise;
- public data test;
- audit calendar
have each been documented.

# Stage 3
## First year of production

### Objective

Demonstrate control under real transactions.

## Month 1

Publish:
- actual production;
- first cargo;
- applicable fiscal terms;
- receipt method.

Verify:
- metering;
- invoice;
- bank receipt;
- accounting entry.

## Quarter 1

Publish:
- production;
- cargo lifts;
- government receipts;
- environmental operating data;
- local employment/procurement with definitions.

Conduct:
- sample cost audit;
- permit inspection;
- spill-response review.

## Quarter 2

Reconcile:
- physical barrels;
- sale barrels;
- royalty;
- profit oil;
- tax;
- fund deposits.

Review:
- contractor cost submission;
- local supplier data;
- grievances.

## Quarter 3

Stress test:
- lower price;
- outage;
- spill;
- project delay.

Update:
- budget assumptions;
- liquidity;
- emergency capacity.

## Quarter 4

Publish:
- audited fund accounts where timing permits;
- annual petroleum account;
- environmental annual report;
- local-content report;
- audit status;
- unresolved items.

No unresolved audit item should disappear from the public record merely because the year closes.

# Stage 4
## Years 2 to 5

### Objective

Keep institutions growing at least as fast as the industry.

## Annual institutional capacity review

Track:
- petroleum-regulator staff;
- engineers;
- auditors;
- environmental specialists;
- tax specialists;
- unresolved audits;
- permit workload;
- production volume.

## Sovereign fund

Annually reconcile:
- opening balance;
- petroleum deposits;
- investment income;
- withdrawals;
- expenses;
- closing balance.

Separate market value and cash.

## Public investment

For every major project:
- appraisal;
- contract;
- cost;
- completion;
- maintenance;
- service outcome.

## Local value

Move from gross procurement to:
- domestic wages;
- domestic inputs;
- retained profit;
- local ownership;
- capability.

## Social baseline

Update:
- household income/consumption;
- prices;
- housing;
- employment;
- education;
- health;
- regional indicators.

Do not rely indefinitely on a pre-oil survey.

## Environmental system

Publish by project:
- emissions;
- flaring;
- discharge;
- incidents;
- inspections;
- permit changes;
- enforcement;
- spill drills.

## Stage 4 gate

At year five conduct an independent public review of:
- legal framework;
- fiscal performance;
- fund accounting;
- public investment;
- environmental outcomes;
- local value;
- household evidence;
- institutional capacity.

The review should propose alternatives rather than assign a political score.

# Crisis playbooks

## A. Oil price falls sharply

Immediate:
- update realized price;
- update petroleum receipts;
- preserve cash/accounting distinction;
- recalculate lawful transfer ceiling;
- review budget exposure.

Within one quarter:
- prioritize essential recurrent spending;
- review project sequencing;
- update debt plan;
- stress fund liquidity.

Do not respond to a temporary price move using an unverified assumption of permanent decline.

## B. Production outage

Immediate:
- establish technical cause;
- update production/cargo schedule;
- estimate cash timing.

Review:
- budget;
- fund deposits;
- contract obligations;
- environmental implications;
- repair logistics.

Separate capacity from actual production during the outage.

## C. Major offshore spill

Immediate:
- protect life;
- activate incident command;
- stop source where feasible;
- notify affected jurisdictions;
- preserve evidence;
- initiate independent sampling.

Financial:
- activate operator liability;
- insurance;
- guarantee;
- emergency public funding if lawfully required.

Public:
- publish verified facts;
- sampling methods;
- response zones;
- claims process.

Do not publish speculative volume as established fact before measurement.

## D. Cost-audit dispute

Maintain:
- amount claimed;
- auditor finding;
- contractor response;
- legal basis;
- procedure;
- final result.

Do not budget a proposed adjustment as recovered money.

## E. Construction overheating

Measure:
- bid counts;
- project delays;
- construction prices;
- wages;
- imports;
- contractor concentration.

Options for public decision include:
- sequencing;
- imported capacity;
- training;
- procurement reform;
- slower rollout.

## F. Natural disaster plus oil-revenue shock

Stress:
- emergency spending;
- lost tax revenue;
- infrastructure damage;
- petroleum downtime;
- fund withdrawals;
- insurance.

This combined case is especially relevant to island producers.

# Island-state adaptation module

## Marine economy

Baseline:
- tourism share;
- fishery share;
- reefs;
- seagrass;
- mangroves;
- beaches;
- desalination.

A marine incident can affect several sectors at once.

## Single-point infrastructure

Map:
- one primary airport;
- one container port;
- one fuel terminal;
- one power station.

Create contingency for concurrent disruption.

## Labour

A small labour force should not adopt unattainable local-content targets.

Prioritize:
- safety;
- technical roles;
- transferable certification;
- supervisory succession;
- supplier capability.

## Revenue concentration

A small state should model petroleum receipts as a share of:
- total revenue;
- GDP;
- exports;
- foreign exchange.

The higher the concentration, the more important a low-price and production-interruption buffer becomes.

# Final decision principles extracted from the Guyana study

1. Prepare legal and financial architecture before recurring revenue.
2. Keep legal versions dated.
3. Keep capacity, production, cargoes and cash separate.
4. Audit recoverable costs.
5. Keep sovereign-fund accounting separate from public-investment outcomes.
6. Keep petroleum emissions and forest/carbon accounting separate.
7. Verify environmental readiness, not permit existence alone.
8. Measure domestic value, not procurement labels alone.
9. Update household evidence after the economic transition.
10. Preserve stakeholder challenge and corrections.
11. Adapt foreign mechanisms to national conditions.
12. Stress-test adverse cases before relying on high-price projections.

# Acceptance checklist for a country using this manual

A country should not describe itself as ready solely because every box is ticked.

The evidence behind each box should be auditable.

Minimum evidence package:
- laws;
- contracts;
- permits;
- audited accounts;
- production data;
- response drill;
- source register;
- stakeholder record;
- corrections log.

The objective is an institutional system capable of learning as evidence changes.
