# TECHNICAL ANNEX C
## Natural Resource Fund reconciliation, fiscal absorption and public-investment conversion

Purpose: provide a transparent framework for moving from petroleum receipts to sovereign financial assets and then to measurable public outcomes. Source IDs refer to SOURCE_REGISTER.md.

## C1. The fund has two separate jobs

A sovereign resource fund can help separate volatile petroleum cash from the annual spending decision, and it can hold financial assets for future use. Those are related but distinct functions.

Guyana's NRF was established by statute before first oil, replaced by a later framework and subsequently amended. [S04, S05, S37] The chronology is important because the design evolved as production and public revenue expanded.

The analysis should not ask only whether a fund exists. It should ask:
- which receipts enter;
- when they enter;
- who holds the cash;
- how it is invested;
- how withdrawals are calculated;
- who authorizes transfers;
- how parliament appropriates spending;
- how accounts are audited;
- how the public can reconcile transactions.

## C2. Stock-flow identity

The core accounting equation is:

Closing financial assets = Opening financial assets + petroleum receipts + investment income + other permitted inflows - lawful withdrawals - permitted expenses +/- valuation movements.

Every annual and quarterly report should disclose each component separately.

A major analytical error occurs when a projected petroleum receipt is added to an observed fund balance. Forecasts belong outside the stock-flow statement until cash is received.

## C3. Cash versus market value

If the fund invests in marketable assets, two values matter:
- cash movement during the period;
- end-period market value.

An unrealized market gain is not a petroleum receipt. Interest and dividend income are not royalties. A security sale can change cash without changing total financial wealth.

The reporting framework should therefore include:
- cash balance;
- securities at market value;
- accrued income;
- receivables;
- realized gain/loss;
- unrealized gain/loss;
- currency effect;
- management expense.

The Bank of Guyana's audited statements and quarterly reports provide the primary record for Guyana's fund. [S44-S47]

## C4. Annual reconciliation protocol

For every fiscal year, build a table with:
1. opening audited balance;
2. profit-oil receipts;
3. royalties;
4. other petroleum receipts;
5. interest;
6. investment gains/losses;
7. withdrawals to the Consolidated Fund;
8. expenses;
9. closing audited balance.

Then reconcile:
- Ministry of Finance petroleum-revenue reporting;
- Bank of Guyana fund reporting;
- parliamentary withdrawal notices;
- audited annual statements.

Differences should be explained by timing, currency, accrual treatment or later restatement.

## C5. 2020 and first receipts

The 2020 audited statement is the first full financial record after first oil and should be treated as the opening historical anchor. [S45] The final paper should identify the first royalty and profit-oil receipts, accounting currency, receivables, cash and any restatement later reported in the 2021 audited statement. [S46]

This historical reconstruction should preserve the audited presentation rather than retrofitting later reporting formats onto earlier years.

## C6. 2021 statutory transition

The 2021 audited account records the replacement of the original 2019 framework by the 2021 statute. [S46] The publication should show:
- legal authority in force at each date;
- who governed the fund;
- investment mandate;
- withdrawal rule;
- reporting requirement;
- role of the central bank;
- oversight arrangements.

A legal change should not be represented as a change in cash until an actual transaction occurs.

## C7. First budget transfer

The IMF's 2023 analysis records the first budget transfer in 2022. [S29] This creates an important case-study line between accumulation and use.

The final paper should trace the first transfer through:
- statutory authorization;
- parliamentary budget;
- central-bank transfer;
- consolidated-fund receipt;
- appropriation;
- spending;
- project or service outcome.

The first four establish legal and financial use. The later stages establish whether the transfer converted into a public asset or service.

## C8. 2024 withdrawal reform

The 2024 amendment changed the withdrawal framework. [S04] A useful legal crosswalk should show the earlier rule beside the amended rule and simulate both using the same petroleum-revenue history.

The comparison should not decide which political choice is preferable. It should show:
- withdrawal capacity under each formula;
- remaining fund balance;
- short-run budget space;
- sensitivity to lower prices;
- implications for future financial assets.

## C9. 2026 observed and projected values

The September 2026 mid-year report provides observed data through June and revised full-year projections. [S07] The final edition should present them in separate columns.

Observed first-half data include government petroleum receipts and fund withdrawals. Projected full-year values remain conditional on prices, production, cost recovery and settlement timing.

The publication should clearly label:
- actual to June;
- revised estimate for the year;
- earlier January estimate;
- variance;
- reason for variance.

## C10. Intergenerational framing

Petroleum is a depleting natural asset. Extracting it converts underground wealth into cash. If all cash is consumed, the national balance sheet loses the resource without gaining an offsetting financial, physical or human asset.

A national-wealth framework therefore asks what replaces the depleted resource:
- foreign financial assets;
- resilient infrastructure;
- human capital;
- competitive firms;
- stronger institutions;
- environmental assets;
- disaster resilience.

This is broader than the fund itself.

## C11. Public investment versus financial saving

A developing producer often faces legitimate infrastructure shortages. Financial saving and domestic investment are therefore competing uses of scarce petroleum wealth, not moral opposites.

The relevant variables include:
- return on external financial assets;
- expected economic return on domestic projects;
- execution capacity;
- maintenance;
- corruption and procurement risk;
- inflationary pressure;
- debt;
- disaster exposure;
- distributional effects.

A domestic project with a high nominal budget does not automatically generate a high social return.

## C12. Absorptive capacity

Absorptive capacity measures how much additional spending the economy can convert into real output before prices, delays or quality problems dominate.

Indicators include:
- construction-cost inflation;
- vacancy rates for engineers and skilled trades;
- contractor backlog;
- port congestion;
- imported material delays;
- contract variation rates;
- project completion times;
- public-agency staffing;
- maintenance backlog.

IMF staff highlighted absorption and public-investment monitoring in 2026. [S64]

## C13. Public-investment gate

Before a petroleum-funded project enters the capital budget, require:
1. problem definition;
2. options analysis;
3. demand forecast;
4. climate/disaster screen;
5. land and social assessment;
6. capital cost;
7. operating and maintenance cost;
8. procurement plan;
9. delivery schedule;
10. responsible operating agency;
11. outcome measure;
12. independent review.

The gate reduces the risk that large cash availability replaces project appraisal.

## C14. Project register

The final flagship should include a model register with:
- project ID;
- sector;
- region;
- sponsoring ministry;
- objective;
- approved cost;
- revised cost;
- funding source;
- procurement route;
- contract date;
- contractor;
- beneficial owner;
- planned completion;
- actual completion;
- commissioning;
- maintenance budget;
- service KPI;
- climate risk;
- audit status.

A project moves from "spent" to "delivered" only after commissioning and service evidence.

## C15. Maintenance as part of wealth preservation

Infrastructure without maintenance converts petroleum wealth into a depreciating asset with a short useful life.

Each major asset should have:
- design life;
- annual maintenance requirement;
- replacement cycle;
- responsible agency;
- budget line;
- condition indicator.

The publication should distinguish new construction from lifecycle funding.

## C16. Debt and the fund

A country can hold a large sovereign fund while also borrowing. The national balance sheet should therefore report:
- gross public debt;
- net debt after liquid financial assets;
- debt currency;
- maturity;
- interest cost;
- contingent liabilities;
- guarantees;
- public-enterprise debt.

Borrowing may be rational for infrastructure, but the comparison should make financing cost and asset return explicit.

## C17. Non-oil primary balance

The non-oil primary balance is one way to measure the fiscal position excluding petroleum revenue and interest expense. IMF staff has discussed its relevance for Guyana. [S64]

The indicator can show whether the non-oil fiscal system is becoming more dependent on petroleum transfers. It should not be used as a political score. It is a diagnostic measure requiring careful treatment of one-off capital spending and economic cycles.

## C18. Fiscal sustainability scenarios

The final model should test:
- oil price at lower, central and higher paths;
- production disruption;
- project delay;
- cost-bank rebuild;
- lower financial returns;
- faster withdrawal;
- higher public investment cost;
- debt-financed infrastructure;
- decommissioning liabilities.

The output should show fund assets, withdrawals, debt and non-oil fiscal balance over at least 10-20 years.

## C19. Spending floor and ceiling tests

A government may wish to protect essential services during a petroleum downturn while preventing expenditure from rising too quickly during a boom.

The analytical model can therefore calculate:
- minimum recurring service cost;
- legally committed transfers;
- capital-project commitments;
- prudent reserve;
- maximum executable capital programme.

These are analytical boundaries, not prescriptions for policy.

## C20. Revenue shock protocol

When projected petroleum revenue falls materially:
1. update the production and price forecast;
2. update contract cost recovery;
3. update fund inflow;
4. test withdrawal rule;
5. identify legally committed spending;
6. rank capital projects by completion value and urgency;
7. publish revised assumptions;
8. avoid hiding the shortfall through optimistic future production.

## C21. Windfall protocol

When revenue materially exceeds forecast:
1. reconcile the source;
2. determine whether the increase is temporary;
3. test capacity constraints;
4. quantify inflation risk;
5. evaluate debt reduction;
6. evaluate additional external saving;
7. evaluate a limited number of high-readiness investments;
8. publish the decision logic.

An unexpected receipt should not automatically become a new recurrent commitment.

## C22. Public-enterprise exposure

Resource states often expand state-owned enterprises or create new entities for energy, transport and infrastructure.

The balance sheet should include:
- direct debt;
- government guarantees;
- purchase agreements;
- availability payments;
- minimum-revenue guarantees;
- fuel or power subsidies;
- pension liabilities;
- off-balance-sheet commitments.

The fund cannot be assessed in isolation from these exposures.

## C23. Climate and disaster exposure

Guyana's coastal geography makes resilience spending relevant to national wealth. The same principle applies differently to islands exposed to hurricanes, storm surge and sea-level rise.

A project evaluation should identify:
- hazard;
- design standard;
- expected lifetime;
- residual risk;
- failure consequence;
- insurance;
- maintenance;
- recovery cost.

A resilient asset may justify higher initial cost if lifecycle risk is lower.

## C24. Human capital

Petroleum wealth can be converted into human capital through education, health and skills, but expenditure alone is not the measure.

Use outcome indicators:
- learning;
- completion;
- technical certification;
- health access;
- mortality;
- specialist staffing;
- labour productivity.

A scholarship count is an input. Completion and labour-market performance are closer to outcomes.

## C25. Regional distribution

A national fund may finance projects across regions with different service gaps.

The public-investment register should record region and beneficiary population. Distribution analysis should use service need and outcomes rather than crude per-capita spending alone.

For hinterland and Indigenous communities, community-level evidence and rights procedures require separate analysis.

## C26. Fund governance controls

The fund operating model should define:
- board duties;
- investment mandate;
- risk limits;
- custodian;
- external manager selection;
- conflict policy;
- audit;
- performance benchmark;
- public report;
- parliamentary notification.

The institutional roles should not overlap ambiguously.

## C27. Investment policy

A fund designed mainly for fiscal stabilization needs higher liquidity than a long-horizon intergenerational fund.

The investment policy should therefore derive from:
- expected withdrawals;
- volatility of petroleum receipts;
- currency needs;
- horizon;
- risk tolerance;
- domestic financial system.

A small new producer should avoid using the sovereign fund as an unstructured domestic development bank unless the law and governance are designed for that role.

## C28. Performance attribution

Fund performance should separate:
- benchmark return;
- active management;
- currency;
- fees;
- cash drag;
- asset allocation.

Investment performance should not be mixed with petroleum inflow growth.

## C29. Transparency dashboard

A quarterly dashboard should publish:
- opening assets;
- petroleum deposits;
- investment income;
- withdrawal;
- ending assets;
- asset allocation;
- benchmark performance;
- fees;
- currency exposure;
- pending withdrawal authorization.

A companion budget dashboard should show how transferred cash is appropriated and spent.

## C30. Outcome verification

For every major petroleum-funded programme, use three measures:
- financial execution;
- physical delivery;
- service outcome.

Example:
A school project can be 100% financially spent, 90% physically complete and 0% operational if staffing and utilities are absent.

This three-part test should appear across infrastructure, health, education, housing and energy.

## C31. New-producer fund readiness test

Before first cash:
- enact fund law;
- appoint lawful custodian;
- adopt investment policy;
- open accounts;
- run synthetic deposit;
- run synthetic withdrawal;
- produce mock quarterly report;
- commission mock audit;
- test parliamentary notice;
- publish the procedures.

The test should fail if agencies rely on manual assumptions that cannot be independently reproduced.

## C32. Ten-year review

A sovereign fund should be reviewed periodically against:
- reserve outlook;
- revenue volatility;
- debt;
- demographics;
- infrastructure stock;
- climate risks;
- public investment performance;
- tax base.

Rules that suited the discovery period may need revision as the country approaches mature production or depletion.

## C33. Annex conclusion

The NRF is one component of national wealth conversion. The stronger case-study test connects petroleum receipts, fund accounting, lawful withdrawals, public investment and service outcomes without collapsing them into one headline.

For a new producer, the operational priority is traceability. Every dollar should have a documented path from petroleum entitlement to the sovereign balance sheet and, where spent, to a measurable public asset or service.
