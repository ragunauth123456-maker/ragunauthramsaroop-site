### Comparative research protocol
For each comparator, the final paper will:
1. use the statute or official fund report as the primary legal source;
2. record the date the mechanism was enacted and the date commercial production began;
3. show actual fund flows for the latest comparable year;
4. separate official objectives from observed fiscal and social outcomes;
5. include at least one independent multilateral or peer-reviewed assessment;
6. identify a failure mode or constraint as well as a documented strength;
7. avoid scores, rankings, winners or political labels.

### Ghana
Ghana's Petroleum Revenue Management Act provides a useful design comparison because it separates near-term stabilization from heritage savings and creates a dedicated public accountability mechanism. The research question is not whether this system is superior to Guyana's. The useful comparison is whether multiple-purpose funds or a single fund with layered rules generate clearer decision rights, fewer off-budget pathways and stronger public comprehension.

### Norway
Norway is relevant for long-horizon financial discipline, integration between petroleum cash and the national budget, professional asset management and extensive disclosure. It is a poor direct template for early state capacity, infrastructure scarcity or immediate development gaps. The flagship will use Norway to illustrate institutional maturity and long-term asset conversion, while clearly stating why a new producer should not import a numerical spending guideline without its own macroeconomic model.

### Timor-Leste
Timor-Leste is a small-state comparison for high petroleum-revenue concentration, public investment needs and the tension between saving and domestic development. Its experience helps test whether a strict benchmark remains credible when infrastructure deficits are large. The research will use updated Petroleum Fund reports before drawing conclusions about current sustainability.

### Suriname
Suriname is the most useful regional pre-first-oil comparison at the 2026 cut-off. It offers a live test of which preparations can be completed before GranMorgu begins production and where national oil company arrangements, fiscal law and public-finance constraints differ. Planned production is not counted as observed output.

### Small-island adaptation
For a small island state, the comparative chapter will add:
- reef and fisheries dependence;
- tourism concentration;
- hurricane and storm-surge overlap;
- desalination or freshwater intake exposure;
- limited ports and response vessels;
- narrow skilled-labour pools;
- smaller domestic supplier markets;
- limited land for shore bases;
- high import shares;
- regional mutual-aid requirements.

The island blueprint therefore emphasizes emergency cooperation, financial assurance, phased local-content targets, offshore savings during construction bottlenecks and independent ecological baselines before first development approval.
