### Purpose
Reconstruct the institutional and commercial sequence behind Guyana's transition to a large offshore petroleum producer. Test where its history supplies documentary evidence for another country preparing for first oil, and where forest endowment, geological scale, fiscal terms and local political institutions limit direct transfer. The intended audience is senior officials, new petroleum regulators, central banks, finance ministries, environmental agencies, civil-society researchers and offshore investors.

### Verified sequence
Guyana has decades of exploration history. Liza was announced as commercially discovered in May 2015. The Stabroek petroleum agreement was entered in June 2016 and the official block register shows an October 2016 effective date. The first Natural Resource Fund law received assent on 23 January 2019, before oil production commenced in December 2019. The 2021 statute replaced the first framework and 2024 legislation changed withdrawal limits. The fourth operating FPSO, Yellowtail, began production in August 2025. Four FPSOs were operating during the first half of 2026. This chronology separates pre-oil institutional decisions from subsequent modifications. [S01-S05, S11]

### September 2026 evidence snapshot
The September 2026 Ministry of Finance mid-year report, describing activity through June, records 162 crude lifts, of which 21 represented government profit oil. During the half-year, government received USD 1,778.6 million in profit-oil proceeds and USD 218.4 million in royalties. The Natural Resource Fund stood at USD 4.2942 billion on June 30 after USD 1.02 billion of first-half withdrawals. The ministry revised its projected full-year 2026 petroleum deposits to USD 6.4976 billion. The latter number is a projection rather than revenue already received. Government estimated overall real GDP growth of 33.3% and non-oil growth of 10.1% for the first half; GDP estimates do not directly show changes in household poverty. [S07, S10]

### The distinction most useful to a new producer
The 2016 Stabroek PSA and Guyana's later model PSA do not have the same fiscal terms. For 2016 Stabroek, the published administrative summary describes a 2% royalty, a cost-recovery ceiling of 75% and equal sharing of profit oil. Under the government revenue authority's simplified binding-cap example, 12.5% profit oil plus 2% royalty yields 14.5% of gross revenue. The figure changes as recoverable costs change. The later model for new licensing announced a 10% royalty, 65% maximum cost recovery and 10% corporate tax. The two regimes must be analysed separately, along with the information available at the time each was negotiated. [S02, S14-S15]

### Institutional subjects for detailed scrutiny
1. Early-law timing. The NRF predates first oil, but the institution changed over time. Assess legislation, actual reporting, parliamentary authorization, investment stewardship and expense outcomes individually.
2. Fiscal and contract administration. Petroleum volumes, government lifts, sales, cost-bank reductions, royalties, cash deposits and budget transfers are different measures. Cost-oil dispute resolution and audit resources matter alongside contract percentages.
3. Environmental assurance. Project-specific EIAs and permits, monitoring, emergency assets, enforceable insurance, operator liability and cross-border response are separate protections. The 2025 pollution act was enacted, but the 2026 mid-year report described implementation regulations and commencement as ongoing work. The May 2026 Court of Appeal decision on financial guarantees requires analysis of the full judgment and underlying instruments.
4. Economic absorption. A high fiscal balance does not establish that roads, hospitals, schools, housing and flood defenses meet service and maintenance targets. IMF staff in July 2026 called for long-run fiscal guideposts, spending-outcome monitoring, procurement compliance and stronger cost-oil audits.
5. Social and community outcomes. Gross domestic supplier spend is not local value added. Old poverty-survey results cannot demonstrate current welfare. Indigenous village allocations require programme-level and locally grounded verification.
6. Transparency and institutional participation. The EITI Board launched Guyana's delayed second validation in May 2026 and recorded concerns about confidence in the independence of civil-society representative selection. The final paper will capture independent stakeholder responses and the final validation decision if published by the cut-off.
7. Parallel climate systems. Guyana's 2009 LCDS and Norway partnership preceded oil. Carbon-credit contracts, payments and village benefit-sharing require separate accounting from the NRF and domestic petroleum emissions. Forest results cannot be transferred mechanically to reef-based or low-forest island states.

### Comparator lenses
Ghana's 2011 revenue law offers distinct stabilization and heritage funds and public publication requirements. Norway's fund documents describe transferring net state petroleum cash into a large investment fund and a long-run real-return-linked fiscal guideline. Timor-Leste's 2005 Petroleum Fund provides another small-state legal design. Suriname's GranMorgu was still under construction in September 2026, with first oil planned for 2028, making the country a relevant pre-production comparison rather than a second operating case. No design is treated as an automatic policy choice for another country. [S24-S27]

### Implementation workbook for a newly discovering jurisdiction
**Exploration:** Record basin geology and uncertainty, licensing authority and contract disclosure, ownership, environmental baseline, marine response capabilities, available technical expertise and prospective contingent liabilities.

**Commercial discovery:** Build independent resource and development assessment, model fiscal terms on common assumptions, identify costs and the risk allocation actually negotiated, obtain project-specific environmental assessment, map nearby fisheries and communities, establish audit rights and publish authorized agreements subject to legitimate protections.

**Before first oil:** Test metering and valuation, recoverable-cost records, royalty invoicing, financial custody, parliamentary procedures, periodic public reporting, spill drills, grievance channels and beneficial-ownership records. Determine budget contingencies without booking forecast oil prices as guaranteed receipts.

**First two years of production:** Publish reconciled lifts, realized prices, royalty and profit-oil cash, fund balances, parliamentary withdrawals, government project execution and all material environmental incidents. Check whether procurement and supplier development result in verified domestic value.

**Scaling:** Stress-test price changes, new project cost-bank additions, maintenance obligations, delayed infrastructure and major spill financial exposure. Compare national growth with independently sourced real household outcomes.
