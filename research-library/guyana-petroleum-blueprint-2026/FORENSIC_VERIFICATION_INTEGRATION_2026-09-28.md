# Forensic verification integration — 28 September 2026

## Purpose

This section incorporates the usable findings from the final forensic review into the reader-facing Edition 2 development PDF. It does not copy the Deep Research report verbatim. Statements that conflict with primary sources or with the dated project register are corrected here rather than propagated.

The evidence cut-off remains **27 September 2026** unless a source is explicitly identified as a 28 September verification source. Observed events, enacted law, commenced law, announced policy, operator or government expectations, estimates and scenarios are kept separate.

## Corrections adopted into the publication

### Petroleum chronology

Commercial production from **Liza Phase 1 began on 20 December 2019**, not in February 2020. **Liza Phase 2** began production on **11 February 2022**. Liza Phase 2 is not Yellowtail. **Payara** began production on **14 November 2023**. **Yellowtail** began production on **8 August 2025**. These dates are treated as observed operating milestones and are distinct from earlier forecasts. [S01, S11, S82-S85]

At the evidence cut-off, the publication does **not** treat Uaru as producing. The Errea Wittu FPSO had arrived in Guyana and commissioning was under way. Government, contractor and operator sources still described first oil as expected later in 2026. Uaru therefore remains **pre-production at the cut-off**. Whiptail remains an approved project under development with startup targeted for 2027. Hammerhead was approved and reached FID on 22 September 2025, with approximately 150,000 bpd design capacity and startup anticipated for 2029. [S124-S128, S144]

Longtail and Haimara are not included in the seven sanctioned-development register. September 2026 government reporting describes Longtail as a **proposed eighth project** and Haimara as a **proposed ninth project** moving through regulatory and environmental review. Their future development, capacity and startup dates therefore remain proposals or forecasts unless and until the relevant approvals are issued. [S145]

### Historical petroleum-capacity record

The early World Bank-supported petroleum programme is now dated more precisely. Credit 1208-GUA was approved in February 1982. The project closed retroactively on 16 September 1987. The World Bank completion report was prepared later and dated in 1993. The report states that 52.1% of the credit was disbursed. The publication therefore avoids describing this as a continuous “1982-1993 project.” The accurate formulation is a **1982-1987 Petroleum Exploration Promotion Project documented in a 1993 completion report**. [S142-S143]

The contemporary 1982 IDA announcement also records that a Petroleum Unit was being established in the Ministry of Energy and Mines and that the programme included technical, legal and accounting assistance plus review of petroleum laws and regulations. These facts strengthen the pre-Liza institutional history without implying that the programme discovered commercial oil. [S142]

### Natural Resource Fund and petroleum revenue

The publication preserves separate accounting categories for government oil entitlement, cargo lifting, sale proceeds, royalties, fund deposits, investment income, withdrawals and closing fund assets. First-half 2026 Ministry of Finance figures remain government-reported interim data, not audited full-year results. The approximately US$184.23 million difference identified in the research ledger remains an **open cash-versus-accrual recognition bridge** until transaction-level reconciliation is complete. [S07, S134-S137]

No sentence should equate a petroleum receipt subtotal with a complete NRF reconciliation. No December 2025 receivable should be counted again as fresh 2026 accrued revenue without a matched transaction record.

### Law, regulation and litigation

The Oil Pollution Prevention, Preparedness, Response and Responsibility Act 2025 is treated as enacted law. Enactment is not automatically treated as proof that every provision, regulation or operational mechanism had commenced by the evidence cut-off. The manuscript keeps commencement and implementing regulations as separate verification questions. [S19]

Court and financial-assurance passages remain attributed where the authenticated full judgment or executed guarantee instrument is not in the evidence pack. Permit conditions requiring insurance, parent or affiliate assurance, monitoring or liability capacity are legal or regulatory requirements. They are **not proof by themselves that every instrument was executed, collectible or independently verified in the amount required**. [S20, S99-S101, S141]

### Transparency and EITI

Guyana's 2026 EITI Validation is described as an ongoing process at the evidence cut-off. The EITI Board's May 2026 decision and call for stakeholder views are reported as institutional records. The manuscript does not convert those records into a political conclusion and does not claim a final 2026 Validation outcome without a later Board decision. [S16-S18]

### Climate policy and carbon accounting

The UNFCCC registry records **Guyana NDC 3.0** as active with a submission date of **1 July 2026**. UNFCCC records the **Low Carbon Development Strategy 2030** as submitted on **19 August 2026** and published on **20 August 2026**. These dates are now the controlling publication-status references. Quantitative NDC targets, Article 6 transfers, REDD+ results, issued credits, sold credits and retired credits must remain separate accounting categories. [S146-S147]

### Environment, households, Indigenous communities and local content

The final forensic review reinforces an evidence rule already used in the manuscript: permit obligations are not the same as observed compliance, absence of a public incident record is not proof that no incident occurred, and a project-level environmental authorization is not a cumulative marine-effects study.

Likewise, business registrations, procurement spend and local-content plans are not equivalent to audited domestic value added, household welfare gains or community outcomes. The 2026 local-content figures remain government- or operator-reported unless independently audited. Current household and Indigenous/community outcome evidence remains too limited for strong causal claims about distribution. [S139-S141]

## Deep Research statements not adopted because primary evidence conflicts

The forensic review output contained several draft statements that are **not** carried into the publication:

- “first oil in February 2020” is rejected. Primary operator evidence places Liza Phase 1 first oil on 20 December 2019.
- “Liza Phase 2 (Yellowtail)” is rejected. Liza Phase 2 is the Liza Unity development. Yellowtail is the fourth development and began production in August 2025.
- Uaru is not backfilled as an observed 2026 startup at the 27 September evidence cut-off. The latest source used here still described startup as expected by year-end.
- Longtail and Haimara are not called sanctioned projects. They remain proposed projects in regulatory or environmental review in the September 2026 evidence set.
- No claim that “all audit issues are resolved” is adopted. The NRF recognition bridge, legal instrument authentication, cost-audit disposition, environmental field evidence, household/community outcomes and external specialist review remain open gates.

## Past, present and future control

The publication now uses three evidence horizons.

**Past:** observed historical records through first oil and the institutional prehistory that preceded Liza. The 1982-1987 World Bank project and 1993 completion report are separated by date and function. [S142-S143]

**Present:** the 27 September 2026 operating position. Four developments are confirmed producing in the source set. Uaru is commissioning/pre-production at the cut-off. Current fiscal, legal, environmental and transparency claims use dated status labels rather than later assumptions. [S11-S19, S124-S141, S144]

**Future:** Whiptail, Hammerhead, Longtail, Haimara, 2030s maturity and 2040s decommissioning are treated according to evidence status. Approved projects are distinguished from proposed projects. Startup dates, production capacities, oil-price paths, fiscal balances and decommissioning outcomes are forecasts or scenarios until observed.

## Remaining publication gates

The PDF is strengthened by this integration but remains an evidence-led development edition until the following are closed: transaction-level H1 2026 NRF reconciliation; later disposition of petroleum cost-audit exceptions; authenticated appellate reasons and financial-assurance instruments where available; cumulative offshore environmental and fisheries evidence; current household and Indigenous/community outcome data; external legal, fiscal, environmental, petroleum-engineering and social review; and final accessibility/design QA.

Those open items are not reasons to suppress the current evidence. They are controls against presenting incomplete evidence as settled fact.
