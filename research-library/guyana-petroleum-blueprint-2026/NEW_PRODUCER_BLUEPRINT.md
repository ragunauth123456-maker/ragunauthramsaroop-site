# New Producer Implementation Blueprint
## A configurable exploration-to-production governance system derived from the Guyana case

### How to use this blueprint
This document converts the Guyana research into a sequence of decisions for a country or island state. It does not prescribe a political choice, ownership structure, fiscal regime or spending ratio. Each government should adapt the sequence to its constitution, legal tradition, geology, marine ecology, population, debt position and institutional capacity.

## Phase 0: Before a commercial discovery

### A. Legal and institutional map
Create one public diagram showing:
- ministry responsible for petroleum policy;
- licensing authority;
- environmental regulator;
- revenue authority;
- central bank;
- auditor general;
- procurement authority;
- maritime safety authority;
- disaster-response command;
- local-government responsibilities;
- Indigenous or customary-rights authorities;
- parliamentary oversight bodies.

For every institution list statutory authority, decision rights, staffing, conflicts-of-interest rules and publication duties.

### B. Geological information governance
Create:
- acreage map;
- licence register;
- seismic and well-data inventory;
- data-room access policy;
- relinquishment schedule;
- beneficial-ownership register for licence holders;
- retention and confidentiality schedule.

Avoid converting exploration forecasts into public-revenue forecasts.

### C. Environmental baseline
Before drilling expands, record:
- currents and seasonal circulation;
- sensitive marine habitats;
- fisheries;
- mangroves, reefs and wetlands where relevant;
- coastal communities and water intakes;
- seabird and marine-mammal observations;
- baseline hydrocarbon chemistry;
- emergency ports and airfields.

Publish versioned datasets where safety and commercial restrictions permit.

### D. Fiscal readiness
Build a transparent petroleum fiscal model with:
- price;
- production;
- development cost;
- operating cost;
- royalty;
- cost-recovery rule;
- profit sharing;
- tax;
- depreciation;
- decommissioning;
- financing;
- timing.

Run downside cases before upside cases.

## Phase 1: Commercial discovery

### Gate 1: Independent discovery review
Before public spending plans rely on oil:
- independently review the resource range;
- distinguish discovery from commerciality;
- publish uncertainty bands;
- require a development plan before treating reserves as production-ready.

### Gate 2: Contract and fiscal review
Use independent petroleum fiscal expertise to model:
- investor breakeven;
- government revenue under low/base/high prices;
- delayed startup;
- cost overruns;
- lower-than-expected reservoir performance;
- decommissioning and abandonment.

Keep negotiation strategy confidential where law permits, but publish executed agreements unless a specific lawful exception applies.

### Gate 3: Sovereign revenue architecture
Before first recurring petroleum receipt:
- enact the revenue-fund legal basis;
- specify deposit and withdrawal rules;
- define investment objectives;
- prohibit off-budget expenditure from the fund unless the constitution expressly provides otherwise;
- appoint accountable fund management;
- define audit frequency;
- specify public reporting;
- create conflict-of-interest rules;
- state how emergency deviations work.

The fund should integrate with the national budget, not become a parallel budget.

## Phase 2: Development approval

### Technical approval packet
Require the operator to submit:
- field development plan;
- reservoir depletion strategy;
- FPSO or platform capacity;
- metering design;
- gas handling;
- flaring controls;
- produced-water management;
- waste plan;
- decommissioning concept;
- local-content plan;
- emergency-response plan.

### Environmental decision packet
Require:
- EIA;
- alternatives analysis;
- cumulative-impact analysis where multiple fields are planned;
- spill modelling;
- sensitive-receptor map;
- consultation record;
- financial assurance;
- monitoring plan;
- incident notification thresholds;
- restoration and compensation procedures.

### Public revenue packet
Before sanction, publish a non-confidential fiscal sensitivity:
- $40 oil;
- $60 oil;
- $80 oil;
- low/base/high production;
- cost-overrun case;
- delay case.

Do not publish a single-point revenue promise.

## Phase 3: Twelve months before first oil

### Revenue rehearsal
Run a simulated month:
1. operator reports production;
2. regulator verifies meters;
3. entitlement is calculated;
4. cargo is lifted;
5. sale price is determined;
6. royalty is invoiced;
7. state profit share is settled;
8. cash reaches the designated account;
9. central bank verifies receipt;
10. sovereign fund statement is published;
11. auditor tests the reconciliation.

Publish the exercise and corrections.

### Emergency rehearsal
Run a live multi-agency spill exercise:
- detection;
- notification;
- command transfer;
- offshore containment;
- shoreline protection;
- fisheries closure;
- independent sampling;
- public communication;
- claims intake;
- cross-border notification.

Record mobilization times and corrective actions.

### Public investment readiness
Create a ranked pipeline of projects, but require:
- economic and social appraisal;
- climate-risk review;
- procurement plan;
- land status;
- maintenance budget;
- delivery agency;
- measurable service output.

Do not equate the size of petroleum receipts with the size of the executable capital programme.

## Phase 4: First oil through rapid scale-up

### Quarterly production-to-cash statement
Publish:
- barrels produced;
- barrels lifted;
- government entitlement;
- realized sale price;
- royalty;
- profit oil;
- tax where applicable;
- sovereign fund deposit;
- investment income;
- withdrawal;
- closing balance.

Use the same definitions each quarter.

### Cost-audit programme
Maintain:
- audit period;
- expenditures tested;
- amount challenged;
- amount accepted;
- amount recovered;
- disputed amount;
- arbitration or court status.

Do not announce proposed disallowances as recovered revenue.

### Local-value ledger
Track:
- supplier beneficial ownership;
- domestic payroll;
- domestic procurement;
- imported component;
- training;
- certifications;
- technology transfer;
- export capability gained.

### Environmental performance ledger
Track by project:
- permit;
- monitoring reports;
- flaring;
- discharges;
- spills;
- enforcement;
- financial-assurance status;
- emergency-drill date.

## Phase 5: Production maturity

### Fiscal transition
Move from startup assumptions toward:
- structural non-resource balance;
- long-term fund-return assumptions;
- public-asset maintenance;
- contingent liabilities;
- post-peak production scenarios.

### Economic transition
Measure:
- productivity outside petroleum;
- non-oil exports;
- energy cost;
- food production;
- manufacturing;
- logistics;
- tourism where relevant;
- workforce participation;
- real household income.

### Resource depletion plan
Before production declines:
- update abandonment funding;
- define post-production regulatory responsibilities;
- model reduced royalties;
- stress-test the national budget;
- determine required financial assets to sustain priority services.

## A special module for small islands

Small islands face conditions that often differ from Guyana.

### Marine concentration risk
A serious spill may affect a large share of fisheries, tourism coastline or desalination infrastructure. Financial assurance and regional response agreements should reflect this concentration.

### Land and labour limits
A small workforce may be unable to supply both petroleum services and existing sectors. Local-content policy should prioritize capability and wage sustainability rather than gross percentage targets alone.

### Fiscal scale
A small public administration may receive revenue far larger than its annual capacity to procure and manage projects. Offshore financial saving becomes more important when rapid domestic spending creates severe inflation or construction bottlenecks.

### Natural-capital differences
Guyana's forest-carbon model depends on its forest endowment and monitoring history. Island comparators should assess mangroves, reefs, seagrass, fisheries and blue-carbon opportunities separately.

### Disaster overlap
Hurricanes, storm surge, coastal erosion and petroleum accidents can coincide. Emergency planning should include compound events.

## Twenty-four-month government action calendar after a commercial discovery

Months 0-3:
- confirm commercial-resource range;
- freeze unsupported revenue promises;
- publish licence chronology;
- appoint independent fiscal/legal/environmental advisers;
- launch baseline environmental update.

Months 4-6:
- publish fiscal-model methodology;
- draft revenue-fund framework;
- create inter-agency petroleum working group;
- publish beneficial-ownership requirements;
- establish contract and cost-data repository.

Months 7-12:
- enact fund and reporting rules;
- complete development EIA;
- define financial assurance;
- simulate first-oil revenue reconciliation;
- establish procurement and local-value baselines.

Months 13-18:
- appoint and train fund oversight bodies;
- conduct spill exercise;
- publish first public investment pipeline;
- test metering and cargo valuation controls;
- train audit teams.

Months 19-24:
- publish readiness audit;
- resolve material gaps before production;
- finalize quarterly disclosure templates;
- establish independent corrections process;
- publish downside fiscal scenarios.

## Five-year scorecard after first oil
The scorecard records evidence, not political grades.

1. Revenue traceability: share of material petroleum receipts reconciled from cargo to sovereign fund.
2. Audit closure: percentage of audited costs with final disposition.
3. Fund transparency: statements published on schedule and externally audited.
4. Project delivery: planned versus completed public infrastructure, plus maintenance readiness.
5. Inflation and absorption: construction costs, wage pressure and execution delays.
6. Local value: domestic wages, ownership and higher-skill services retained.
7. Environment: permit compliance, incidents, spill drills and assurance coverage.
8. Social outcomes: current household survey, water, health, education and regional access.
9. Economic diversification: non-oil exports and productivity.
10. Intergenerational balance: financial assets, liabilities and decommissioning commitments.

## Red flags
- budgeting against undiscovered or unsanctioned reserves;
- a sovereign fund with no binding publication rule;
- fund spending outside the normal budget;
- a single-point oil-price forecast;
- environmental permitting after commercial commitments become difficult to reverse;
- local-content targets without supplier ownership checks;
- gross procurement presented as domestic value added;
- carbon-credit contracts presented as immediate cash;
- production capacity presented as actual output;
- an enacted environmental statute presented as operational before commencement and regulations;
- an unresolved audit dispute presented as revenue already recovered;
- public investment measured by money spent rather than services working.

## Final adaptation rule
Copy the decision discipline, not the exact Guyanese numbers.

Every country should ask which Guyana practice solved a general governance problem, which depended on Guyana's own natural endowment, and which remains under evaluation. The strongest blueprint is one that preserves this distinction.
