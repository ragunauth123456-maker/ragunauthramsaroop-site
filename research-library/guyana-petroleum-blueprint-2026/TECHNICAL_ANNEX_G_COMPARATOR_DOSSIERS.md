# TECHNICAL ANNEX G
## Comparator implementation dossiers: Norway, Ghana, Timor-Leste, Suriname, Trinidad and Tobago, and small-island states

Purpose: compare institutional mechanisms without ranking political systems or selecting a preferred country model.

## G1. Comparator protocol

Every country case uses the same questions:
1. What stage of petroleum development?
2. What is the revenue-management vehicle?
3. How are withdrawals linked to the budget?
4. What is the investment policy?
5. Which institutions audit and publish?
6. How concentrated is petroleum revenue?
7. What is the reserve/depletion profile?
8. What is the non-oil tax base?
9. What environmental exposure is distinctive?
10. Which elements are transferable?

The protocol prevents a large mature producer from being compared to a new island producer through headline fund size alone.

## G2. Norway

Norway's Government Pension Fund Global operates within a mature fiscal and institutional system. Official publications describe the transfer of net state petroleum cash to the fund and a fiscal guideline associated with the expected long-run real return. [S25, S74]

Implementation features:
- petroleum revenue is separated from ordinary budget revenue;
- fund assets are invested largely abroad;
- withdrawals enter the national budget;
- performance is benchmarked;
- parliamentary and public reporting is extensive.

Transfer constraints:
- decades of institutional development;
- large non-oil tax base;
- mature capital markets;
- very large accumulated fund;
- strong administrative capacity.

New-producer adaptation:
Use budget integration, transparent reporting and external diversification as design references. Do not copy the numerical spending rule without modeling local infrastructure deficits, revenue volatility and absorption.

## G3. Ghana

Ghana's Petroleum Revenue Management Act established stabilization and heritage purposes. Bank of Ghana reporting provides repeated fund statements. [S24, S75-S76]

Implementation features:
- statutory fund structure;
- stabilization function;
- heritage function;
- central-bank management;
- recurring reporting.

Transfer constraints:
- different reserve trajectory;
- different fiscal regime;
- different currency and debt environment;
- different production maturity.

New-producer adaptation:
Define stabilization and intergenerational purposes separately even if one legal fund is used. Publish allocation rules and exception procedures.

## G4. Timor-Leste

Timor-Leste's Petroleum Fund provides a small-state comparison. The central bank publishes monthly, quarterly and annual reporting and describes liquidity and growth portfolios. [S26, S77-S78]

Implementation features:
- transparent central-bank reporting;
- financial asset accumulation;
- formal withdrawal framework;
- investment portfolios linked to liquidity needs.

Core risk:
A well-managed financial portfolio does not eliminate fiscal dependence on withdrawals if petroleum production declines and the domestic tax base remains narrow.

New-producer adaptation:
Model the post-field budget before first oil. A permanent public salary or subsidy should survive a conservative production and return scenario.

## G5. Suriname

Suriname's GranMorgu project had reached final investment decision and remained under construction at the September 2026 source cut-off, with first oil targeted for 2028. [S27, S79]

Implementation relevance:
- regional geology;
- offshore deepwater development;
- pre-first-oil preparation period;
- national oil company role;
- potential regional services and emergency cooperation.

Transfer constraint:
Staatsolie gives Suriname a different institutional structure from Guyana. The state-company role changes access to commercial information and internal technical capability.

New-producer adaptation:
A country without a national oil company should not assume the same information flows. It needs explicit regulatory data rights and independent expertise.

## G6. Trinidad and Tobago

Trinidad and Tobago is a mature Caribbean petroleum and gas producer with a Heritage and Stabilisation Fund reporting framework. [S80]

Implementation relevance:
- long experience with commodity cycles;
- petroleum and gas industry capabilities;
- regional professional services;
- fiscal stabilization;
- industrial downstream history.

Transfer constraint:
Its established petrochemical base, infrastructure and long production history are not available to a newcomer.

New-producer adaptation:
Study mature-cycle risk, stabilization and industrial capability. Test downstream proposals against market scale and delivered feedstock economics.

## G7. Guyana as a comparator in its own right

Guyana's distinctive features include:
- rapid sequence from 2015 discovery to 2019 first oil;
- large discoveries in a small economy;
- production scale-up through multiple FPSOs;
- NRF law before first oil;
- evolving fund rules;
- local-content legislation after production started;
- pre-existing LCDS and forest finance;
- low coastal geography and major hinterland forest estate.

These features make the case rich but limit automatic transfer.

## G8. Small-island producer archetype

Assume a hypothetical island with:
- population below one million;
- tourism above 30% of exports;
- limited skilled workforce;
- sensitive reefs;
- hurricane exposure;
- no large sovereign fund;
- narrow tax base;
- one commercial offshore field.

The preferred analysis is not "Which country should it copy?" It is "Which mechanism fits each constraint?"

## G9. Fund design for the island archetype

Potential requirements:
- high liquidity reserve for production interruption and disasters;
- external investment to limit domestic overheating;
- conservative recurrent-spending commitments;
- explicit post-field fiscal plan;
- transparent budget integration.

A heritage component may be useful, but the share is a sovereign policy choice.

## G10. Environmental design for the island archetype

Priority capabilities:
- reef and fishery baseline;
- tourism-impact plan;
- hurricane-plus-spill exercise;
- transboundary agreements;
- rapid claims process;
- regional equipment access;
- desalination protection.

These differ from Guyana's forest-heavy natural-capital profile.

## G11. Local-content design for the island archetype

Avoid setting statutory percentages that exceed workforce and supplier capacity.

Prioritize:
- safety-critical training;
- professional services;
- environmental monitoring;
- marine logistics;
- data;
- transferable technical skills.

Measure domestic value added rather than registrations.

## G12. Comparator metrics

Use common metrics:
- petroleum revenue/non-oil GDP;
- fund assets/non-oil GDP;
- withdrawals/fund assets;
- non-oil tax/non-oil GDP;
- debt/non-oil GDP;
- production per capita;
- reserve life;
- capital spending/non-oil GDP;
- current-account balance;
- inflation;
- non-resource exports;
- environmental response time.

Common metrics improve comparison without creating a political ranking.

## G13. Institutional maturity dimensions

Describe, do not score:
- legal clarity;
- data availability;
- independent audit;
- budget integration;
- environmental monitoring;
- dispute resolution;
- public participation;
- statistical freshness.

A qualitative narrative is preferable to a single composite grade.

## G14. Transfer decision record

For each proposed borrowed policy, document:
- source country;
- policy;
- original stage;
- prerequisite;
- receiving-country gap;
- adaptation;
- cost;
- risk;
- review date.

Example:
"External sovereign-fund investment" may transfer from Norway, but the receiving country may need a larger liquid reserve and a lower risk tolerance.

## G15. Comparator failure modes

Common errors:
- selecting Norway because of fund size without adjusting for institutional history;
- selecting Ghana because legal language appears familiar without modeling local revenue volatility;
- selecting Timor-Leste without examining depletion dependence;
- treating Suriname's planned first oil as observed success;
- assuming Trinidad's downstream industry is reproducible at smaller scale;
- copying Guyana's forest-carbon arrangements into an island with different ecosystems.

## G16. Comparator chapter output

Each country profile in the final PDF should include:
- two-page institutional summary;
- fiscal-flow diagram;
- fund/budget relationship;
- key risks;
- transfer limits;
- dated sources.

The purpose is practical comparison.

## G17. Annex conclusion

Comparators are strongest when they reveal prerequisites and failure modes. The new producer should borrow mechanisms selectively, preserve local democratic decision-making and publish why each adaptation was made.
