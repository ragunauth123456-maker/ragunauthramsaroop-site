# PART VI RESEARCH DOSSIER
## Local Content, Domestic Value, Workforce, Household Welfare and Indigenous/Hinterland Outcomes

Working research dossier for Chapters 28-33 of the 520-560-page flagship publication.  
Evidence cut-off: 27 September 2026.  
Status: research stage. Independent labour, household-economics and Indigenous-rights review pending.

# Research objective

Petroleum development affects national welfare through several channels:
- government revenue;
- direct employment;
- supplier contracts;
- wages;
- training;
- public investment;
- price changes;
- land and housing markets;
- infrastructure;
- community programmes;
- environmental effects.

These channels should not be collapsed into one local-content expenditure number.

The final flagship will distinguish:
- procurement booked to a Guyanese supplier;
- local beneficial ownership;
- imported content;
- domestic labour;
- domestic wages;
- domestic profit retained;
- domestic manufacturing or technical value added;
- training;
- permanent capability after a contract ends.

# Chapter 28 research dossier
## Local content law and implementation

Parliament records the Local Content Act 2021 as Act 18 of 2021, passed on 29 December 2021 and published on 4 January 2022. [S57]

The official Local Content Register provides a system for supplier certification and employment registration. [S58]

The final legal chapter should extract:
- statutory definition of Guyanese national;
- definition of Guyanese company;
- ownership and management requirements;
- scheduled services and targets;
- local-content plans;
- reporting frequency;
- supplier registration;
- employment requirements;
- capacity-development requirements;
- audit and inspection powers;
- offences and penalties;
- ministerial/regulatory powers.

Every statutory target should be recorded with the exact schedule and date in force.

## Reporting evolution

Government mid-year reporting documents changes in implementation and reporting scope. The 2025 report states that half-year reporting guidelines were expanded to capture procurement activity in ten additional sectors and subsectors already identified in the legislation, with local-capacity assessment informing future targets. [S60]

The final paper should explain when reporting scope changes, because a rising reported procurement figure may partly reflect wider reporting coverage rather than only underlying economic growth.

# Chapter 29 research dossier
## Domestic value added versus local procurement

A supplier invoice does not reveal how much value stays in the country.

Consider four stylized cases:

### Case A: imported item, local reseller
A Guyanese supplier imports a complete product and resells it. Domestic value may consist mainly of local wages, warehousing, financing and margin.

### Case B: imported inputs, local fabrication
Raw material is imported but cutting, welding, engineering, testing and maintenance occur locally. Domestic value is higher.

### Case C: local service
A locally owned engineering, logistics or professional-services firm supplies labour and expertise primarily from Guyana.

### Case D: foreign-controlled local operation
A locally incorporated company employs Guyanese workers but profit, intellectual property and management services are largely external.

All four may contribute to the economy, but they should not be reported as economically identical.

## Domestic value-added formula

The final research model should estimate:

Gross local-content procurement  
- imported goods embedded in those purchases  
- imported subcontracted services  
- foreign management/IP charges attributable to delivery  
= estimated domestic gross value added.

Then split domestic value into:
- wages;
- local operating surplus;
- taxes;
- domestic intermediate inputs.

This requires customs, tax, ownership and firm-level evidence.

## Ownership verification

The local-content register should be sampled against:
- beneficial ownership;
- shareholder records;
- management control;
- bank/payment destination;
- payroll;
- customs imports;
- related-party contracts.

Certification status should remain separate from independent economic measurement.

# Chapter 30 research dossier
## Workforce and skills

Government petroleum reporting provides employment counts, but a durable skills analysis needs occupational detail.

The final workforce dataset should include:
- occupation;
- nationality;
- gender;
- age;
- region;
- permanent/temporary;
- offshore/onshore;
- employer;
- wage band;
- certification;
- training;
- promotion;
- retention;
- safety outcomes.

## Skills pipeline

Map:
secondary school  
→ technical/vocational training  
→ apprenticeship  
→ entry role  
→ technical certification  
→ supervisor  
→ professional/management role.

The oil sector may increase returns to technical training while also drawing engineers and tradespeople away from construction, mining, utilities and public agencies.

A national assessment should therefore measure both petroleum-sector employment and vacancies/wage pressure elsewhere.

## Training outcomes

Training expenditure is an input.

Outcome indicators include:
- certificates completed;
- internationally recognized qualifications;
- post-training employment;
- wage progression;
- retention;
- local succession into positions previously expatriate;
- safety performance.

# Chapter 31 research dossier
## Household welfare and distribution

The Bureau of Statistics states that the Household Budget and Living Condition Survey fieldwork ran from November 2018 through November 2019 across all ten administrative regions. [S61]

This creates a major evidence boundary.

A pre-first-oil or first-oil-era household survey should not be reported as a 2026 poverty or living-standard measurement.

The final social chapter should build a timeline of available household evidence and label the latest observation year for every indicator.

## Welfare dashboard

Required measures:
- real household consumption;
- income;
- food expenditure share;
- housing cost;
- electricity access and reliability;
- water and sanitation;
- internet;
- transport;
- school attendance/completion;
- health access;
- employment;
- regional inequality;
- gender;
- hinterland/coastal differences.

Macroeconomic GDP growth should appear separately.

## Price effects

Rapid investment and public expenditure may affect:
- rent;
- land;
- construction materials;
- food;
- transport;
- skilled labour;
- services.

A household can face rising nominal income and rising costs at the same time.

The final analysis should therefore use real purchasing power rather than nominal income alone.

# Chapter 32 research dossier
## Amerindian and hinterland development

Indigenous and hinterland outcomes arise through more than petroleum.

Relevant systems include:
- land titling;
- public infrastructure;
- health and education;
- telecommunications;
- renewable/off-grid energy;
- carbon-credit benefit sharing;
- public employment;
- resource governance.

The LCDS programme states that 15% of carbon-credit revenues are made available for village-led plans and describes a village consultation/planning process. [S62, S63]

The final paper should distinguish:
- government programme design;
- village decision;
- funds allocated;
- funds received;
- project procured;
- project completed;
- project operating;
- measured community outcome.

## Consent and participation

The research should seek evidence from:
- National Toshaos' Council;
- individual village councils;
- Indigenous organizations;
- legal cases;
- independent rights specialists;
- UN/other safeguard records.

Government programme descriptions should not substitute for affected-community evidence.

The chapter should also record disagreement where consultation, land rights or benefit-sharing processes are contested.

# Chapter 33 research dossier
## Social licence, grievance and participation

A large national development programme needs mechanisms for affected people to challenge decisions.

The final institutional map should include:
- EPA public-comment process;
- petroleum/ministry grievance mechanisms;
- judicial review;
- EITI multi-stakeholder engagement;
- village governance;
- procurement complaints;
- labour complaints;
- local-content supplier complaints.

For each mechanism:
- who has standing;
- submission process;
- deadline;
- decision maker;
- publication rule;
- appeal;
- observed case duration.

## Evidence versus sentiment

Public opinion surveys, if methodologically sound, may measure attitudes.

They should not substitute for:
- statutory rights;
- environmental results;
- actual household outcomes;
- audited financial flows.

The flagship should report them as a separate evidence category.

# Longitudinal local-content evidence

Government reports should be transformed into a consistent time series with fields for:
- reporting companies;
- sectors covered;
- gross procurement;
- training expenditure;
- local hires;
- total Guyanese employment;
- methodology changes.

For example, the 2024 Mid-Year Report described reporting from 40 companies, H1 procurement of US$314 million, more than 454 new hires during the review period and 4,467 Guyanese employed by those reporting companies, while the government estimated total Guyanese oil-industry employment at about 6,500. [S59]

These figures are government-reported and should be retained with their stated population and reporting scope.

They should not be merged with later periods unless definitions are stable.

# Local-value audit sample

The flagship should propose a stratified sample of suppliers across:
- engineering;
- fabrication;
- logistics;
- marine;
- aviation;
- catering;
- accommodation;
- transportation;
- legal/accounting;
- environmental services;
- manpower;
- equipment rental.

For each sampled contract:
1. contract value;
2. imported goods;
3. imported services;
4. Guyanese payroll;
5. domestic purchases;
6. taxes;
7. retained local profit;
8. training;
9. capital equipment left in country;
10. capability gained.

This produces a more meaningful measure of domestic value than invoice totals alone.

# Distributional analysis

Petroleum benefits and costs should be mapped by:
- region;
- urban/rural;
- coastal/hinterland;
- income group;
- gender;
- age;
- occupation.

The final paper should avoid an unsupported statement that national gains were evenly distributed.

Likewise, absence of current household data should not be used to infer poor outcomes.

Where evidence is unavailable, state the gap.

# Publication boundary

The paper will not equate gross local procurement with domestic value added.

It will not equate petroleum GDP with household welfare.

It will not equate a programme allocation with a verified community outcome.

Each will be measured on its own evidence.
