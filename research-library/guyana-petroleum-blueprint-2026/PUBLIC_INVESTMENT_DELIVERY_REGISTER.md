# PUBLIC INVESTMENT DELIVERY REGISTER
## Petroleum-era resource conversion control

This register is intentionally stricter than a budget table.

A project enters the register at appropriation or contract stage, but it is not classified as a delivered national asset until there is evidence of commissioning. It is not classified as a demonstrated public-service outcome until operating evidence exists.

### Delivery stages

1. **Allocated**: budget authority exists.
2. **Contracted**: procurement and contract evidence exists.
3. **Under construction**: physical progress evidence exists.
4. **Commissioned**: completion/acceptance evidence exists.
5. **Operational**: asset is staffed, powered, maintained and in service.
6. **Outcome evidenced**: travel time, reliability, health access, flood protection or other service indicator shows measurable effect.

### Why this belongs in the petroleum book

NRF withdrawals enter the Consolidated Fund and are used through the national budget. The analytical chain cannot stop at the withdrawal. A durable resource-conversion assessment must follow public money into assets and outcomes.

The 2024 Auditor General report provides an important counterweight to budget announcements because it shows why contract execution, overpayments, documentation and asset maintenance matter after appropriation. [S130]

The 2026 budget provides a broad map of planned public investment, but the register deliberately separates those allocations from completed outcomes. [S129]

### Audit principle

The book must not say "petroleum revenue built X" merely because X appears in a petroleum-era budget. Funding sources can be fungible, projects can be financed by loans or grants, and budget execution can differ from appropriation.

Where a direct petroleum-funding link is not documented, the wording should be "public investment during the petroleum-revenue period" or "budget supported in part by NRF transfers" only when the public accounts establish that relationship.
