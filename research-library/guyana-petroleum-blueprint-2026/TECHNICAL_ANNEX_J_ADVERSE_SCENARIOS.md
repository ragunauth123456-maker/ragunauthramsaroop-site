# TECHNICAL ANNEX J
## Adverse scenarios, combined shocks and decision playbooks

Purpose: test whether resource-governance institutions continue functioning when assumptions fail. The scenarios are analytical exercises, not predictions.

## J1. Scenario architecture

Every scenario has:
- trigger;
- direct effect;
- fiscal channel;
- operational channel;
- environmental/social channel;
- decision owner;
- immediate actions;
- medium-term actions;
- public disclosure;
- recovery test.

## J2. Oil price shock

Assume realized oil price falls 40% for two years.

Test:
- gross revenue;
- royalty;
- profit oil;
- fund deposit;
- withdrawal rule;
- capital programme;
- debt;
- suppliers.

Do not multiply government revenue by -40% mechanically. Cost recovery and production-sharing rules interact.

## J3. Production outage

Assume one major FPSO is unavailable for 90 days.

Test:
- lost barrels;
- government entitlement;
- cargo schedule;
- supplier activity;
- budget forecast;
- maintenance cause.

The scenario should identify whether insurance affects government cash.

## J4. Delayed new project

Assume first oil slips two years.

Remove the project from:
- production forecast;
- petroleum revenue;
- local-content demand;
- port forecast;
- power demand.

Do not smooth the delay into a lower annual growth rate without showing the missing project.

## J5. Cost-bank rebuild

Assume a new sanctioned development materially increases recoverable cost.

Test government profit oil under:
- high cost recovery;
- declining cost recovery;
- later plateau.

This scenario prevents a high-receipt year from being treated as permanent.

## J6. Audit dispute

Assume a large cost audit produces a proposed adjustment.

Record:
- proposed amount;
- operator response;
- contractual procedure;
- expected timing;
- no immediate cash recovery unless settled.

The budget should not spend a disputed amount.

## J7. Lower fund investment returns

Assume global markets deliver negative returns for a year.

Test:
- fund market value;
- liquidity;
- withdrawal;
- budget reliance.

Petroleum deposits and investment returns should remain separate.

## J8. Higher inflation

Assume construction inflation exceeds CPI by 10 percentage points.

Test:
- project cost;
- variation;
- completion;
- maintenance;
- real public-investment volume.

A larger nominal budget may buy less infrastructure.

## J9. Labour shortage

Assume technical vacancies double.

Test:
- regulator inspections;
- audit backlog;
- project schedule;
- contractor wages;
- public-service retention.

Potential responses include compensation, training and targeted external recruitment. The paper does not prescribe one.

## J10. Currency pressure

Assume domestic prices rise relative to trading partners.

Test:
- non-oil exports;
- imports;
- wages;
- tourism competitiveness.

The scenario focuses on real, not only nominal, exchange conditions.

## J11. Major spill

Assume a severe offshore release during adverse weather.

Test:
- notification;
- command;
- capping;
- equipment;
- fisheries closure;
- transboundary notification;
- financial assurance;
- claims;
- independent sampling.

The exercise should use a range of release volumes rather than one false-precision estimate.

## J12. Financial-assurance shortfall

Assume immediate cleanup cost exceeds readily available security.

Test:
- operator liquidity;
- insurance;
- parent support;
- state emergency spending;
- recovery rights.

This separates legal liability from cash available in the first weeks.

## J13. Hurricane plus spill for an island

Assume:
- airport closure;
- damaged port;
- telecom outage;
- oil release.

Test regional response agreements and redundant logistics.

This combined scenario is more relevant to some islands than Guyana.

## J14. Forest-carbon price shock

Assume carbon-credit demand or price weakens.

Test:
- expected climate-finance receipts;
- village allocation;
- LCDS programmes.

Do not transfer the shortfall to petroleum accounts.

## J15. EITI or disclosure delay

Assume a required report is late.

Test:
- legal deadline;
- cause;
- interim data;
- stakeholder communication;
- corrective action.

A transparency system should have a late-report protocol.

## J16. Procurement failure

Assume a major petroleum-funded infrastructure contract has:
- 30% cost overrun;
- one-year delay;
- weak maintenance plan.

Test whether the public-investment framework pauses similar projects or continues unchanged.

## J17. Public-enterprise liability

Assume a state enterprise requires a fiscal transfer.

Add the exposure to the national balance sheet and test fund-withdrawal pressure.

## J18. Debt-market shock

Assume borrowing cost rises sharply.

Test:
- refinancing;
- capital programme;
- fund assets;
- debt reduction option.

A resource fund and debt strategy must be analyzed together.

## J19. Household cost-of-living shock

Assume food and housing prices rise faster than wages for lower-income households.

Test:
- real consumption;
- transfer policy cost;
- housing response;
- survey evidence.

GDP growth should not be used to dismiss the distributional effect.

## J20. Local-content fraud risk

Assume evidence emerges that nominal local firms are controlled by foreign beneficial owners.

Test:
- certification;
- enforcement;
- procurement contracts;
- remediation;
- future verification.

A suspected case should be investigated, not generalized to the entire programme.

## J21. Data-system failure

Assume production, fund and budget data disagree.

Freeze publication of a consolidated headline until reconciliation identifies:
- timing;
- currency;
- scope;
- error.

Publish the correction.

## J22. Political transition

A change in administration should not break core records.

Test:
- data custody;
- contract archive;
- fund accounts;
- regulator files;
- audit continuity.

The exercise is institutional and does not assume any electoral outcome.

## J23. Operator exit

Assume a contractor sells its interest.

Test:
- assignment approval;
- new owner capability;
- decommissioning liability;
- financial assurance;
- tax;
- data transfer.

## J24. Decommissioning shock

Assume end-of-life cost doubles.

Test:
- reserve;
- contractor security;
- state exposure.

This scenario should run long before cessation.

## J25. Combined fiscal shock

Combine:
- oil at 40;
- production -15%;
- cost recovery at ceiling;
- negative fund return;
- high construction inflation.

This is the severe fiscal case.

## J26. Combined environmental-economic shock

Combine:
- spill;
- tourism or fisheries loss;
- cleanup;
- project outage;
- lower fund inflow.

This shows why environmental risk is also fiscal risk.

## J27. Recovery indicators

After a shock, monitor:
- production restoration;
- fund reconciliation;
- project reprioritization;
- environmental recovery;
- claims;
- household indicators.

A response is not complete when the press release ends.

## J28. Decision log

Each crisis decision should record:
- time;
- authority;
- evidence;
- option;
- reason;
- review date.

The log supports later audit.

## J29. Exercise programme

Run annually:
- fiscal shock;
- spill;
- cyber/data failure.

Run every two years:
- combined national exercise.

Publish corrective actions.

## J30. Annex conclusion

Institutions are best tested under failure. A newcomer should design stress tests before the first crisis and update them as production, the sovereign fund and public spending scale.
