# TECHNICAL ANNEX A
## Exploration-to-production chronology, legal version control and institutional crosswalk

Purpose: provide a reproducible chronology for analysts, regulators and governments using Guyana as a case study. Source IDs refer to SOURCE_REGISTER.md. Dates in this annex distinguish discovery, signature, effective date, assent, publication, commencement, first production and cash receipt.

## A1. Why chronology is a control, not a narrative convenience

Resource-governance analysis becomes unreliable when events from different legal periods are merged. A contract signed in one fiscal environment should not be evaluated as though later model terms applied to it. A statute that was passed after first oil should not be described as a pre-production safeguard. A carbon-finance programme that began years before petroleum production should not be portrayed as a response to the oil boom. Guyana's case therefore needs a strict version-controlled chronology.

The chronology has six date types. The first is geological, such as an exploration well or commercial discovery. The second is contractual, such as signature or effective date. The third is statutory, which may include passage, assent, publication and commencement. The fourth is administrative, such as a production licence or environmental permit. The fifth is operational, such as first oil or a production milestone. The sixth is financial, such as receipt, deposit, investment return or withdrawal.

A robust database should store these as separate fields. One free-text "date" field is insufficient. The Stabroek record illustrates the problem: the petroleum agreement was signed in June 2016, while the official block register records a later effective date. [S02, S03, S41] The NRF also requires several dates because the 2019 law was later replaced in 2021 and withdrawal provisions changed again in 2024. [S04, S05, S37]

## A2. Pre-discovery institutional baseline

### Petroleum law and historical exploration

Guyana's petroleum exploration history reaches well before Liza. The official history identifies licence activity from the twentieth century, including onshore and offshore exploration, investment-promotion efforts and periods of interruption. [S35] The 1986 Petroleum (Exploration and Production) Act provided an important legal foundation for exploration and production rights before the modern offshore boom. [S38]

The analytical implication is that the modern petroleum industry did not emerge into a legal vacuum. Any assessment of post-2015 capacity should identify which powers, offices and procedures already existed and which were created or materially strengthened later. This avoids attributing all institutional development to the discovery itself.

### Environmental law

The Environmental Protection Act 1996 established the Environmental Protection Agency and an environmental-impact framework before the petroleum discovery. [S39] The pre-discovery baseline therefore includes statutory environmental authority, even though offshore petroleum later required project-specific permits, impact assessments and technical capability far beyond the general statute.

A new producer should run the same baseline test. Before drafting petroleum-specific environmental provisions, it should identify existing environmental, maritime, fisheries, disaster-response, planning, labour, tax and customs law. Overlapping authority should be resolved before a commercial discovery produces time pressure.

### Low-carbon development

Guyana's first Low Carbon Development Strategy began in 2009, and the Guyana-Norway results-based forest partnership operated during the pre-oil era. [S22] This matters for historical causality. Forest monitoring, climate-finance relationships and aspects of national development strategy preceded first oil. Petroleum revenue and forest-carbon finance later coexisted, but one should not be described as the origin of the other.

## A3. Discovery and contract period, 2015-2016

The Liza-1 well record identifies the May 2015 exploration event in the Stabroek Block. [S36] Operator historical reporting also identifies the commercial discovery and subsequent development sequence. [S01]

The 2016 Stabroek petroleum agreement belongs to the discovery-to-development phase. Its terms should be evaluated against the executed agreement and the information available at the time. [S02] Later discoveries and production success can inform retrospective analysis, but they do not alter the uncertainty that existed at signature.

The legal record should retain at least these fields:
- block;
- parties;
- agreement signature date;
- effective date;
- licence date;
- work obligations;
- relinquishment terms;
- fiscal provisions;
- cost-recovery provisions;
- valuation provisions;
- tax provisions;
- audit rights;
- decommissioning duties;
- transfer and assignment provisions;
- environmental obligations;
- dispute-resolution mechanisms;
- amendment history.

This crosswalk prevents analysts from quoting one fiscal term without showing the wider contractual system.

## A4. Discovery-to-first-oil institution building, 2016-2019

A major part of the Guyana case is the use of the period before recurring petroleum cash to design revenue-management institutions. The pre-oil sovereign wealth fund Green Paper documents technical work beginning in the years after discovery, including inter-agency preparation and external technical support. [S28]

The NRF Act 2019 was enacted before first oil. [S37] This is a concrete timing fact. The more demanding question is whether the entire operational architecture, including banking, reporting, investment, withdrawal authorization and audit, was ready for the first material receipts. That question should be answered through financial statements and transaction records rather than the date of the law alone. [S44-S47]

The period also included project-level environmental approvals for the first developments. Liza Phase 1 permit records document the environmental authorization framework and later modifications. [S48] This gives the chronology a second institutional track alongside finance.

## A5. First oil and early production, 2019-2021

Liza Phase 1 began production in December 2019. [S01, S83] First oil is operationally distinct from the first cash deposit, because crude must be produced, lifted, sold, invoiced and settled before cash reaches the sovereign account.

The early production period should therefore record:
- first production date;
- first government entitlement;
- first government cargo;
- invoice date;
- settlement date;
- first NRF deposit;
- first audited annual account;
- first parliamentary withdrawal;
- first cost audit covering producing years.

The IMF's 2023 report states that first petroleum receipts entered the fund in 2020 and the first budget transfer occurred in 2022. [S29] These events demonstrate why "first oil" and "first fiscal use" should never be treated as the same date.

In 2021, the NRF framework was replaced and the Local Content Act was enacted. [S05, S57] Those are post-first-oil reforms. A chronology-based case study should describe them as institutional adaptation during early production rather than safeguards that existed before the first barrel.

## A6. Rapid scale-up, 2022-2026

Liza Phase 2 began production in February 2022. [S82] Payara followed in November 2023. [S83] Yellowtail began in August 2025. [S84] By 2026, four FPSOs were operating and the government's production dashboard provided a monthly production series through July. [S81]

This sequence means regulatory and fiscal workload expanded quickly. Every new development adds permit conditions, production metering, cost-recovery claims, decommissioning obligations, emergency-response requirements and public revenue flows. The administrative burden scales faster than a simple count of FPSOs suggests.

The 2024 NRF withdrawal amendment belongs to this mature-early-production phase, not the pre-first-oil design. [S04] Likewise, the 2025 oil-pollution statute belongs to a period when multiple projects were already operating or under development. [S53-S54]

## A7. Legal version-control table

For every legal instrument, the final publication should carry a version table with:
- title;
- bill or act number;
- parliamentary passage date;
- assent date;
- publication date;
- commencement date;
- amendment dates;
- repealed provisions;
- responsible minister or agency;
- regulations issued;
- current status at the evidence cut-off;
- authoritative source URL.

The following Guyana instruments are mandatory in the final crosswalk:
1. Petroleum (Exploration and Production) Act 1986. [S38]
2. Environmental Protection Act 1996. [S39]
3. Natural Resource Fund Act 2019. [S37]
4. Natural Resource Fund Act 2021 and 2024 amendment record. [S04-S05]
5. Local Content Act 2021. [S57]
6. Petroleum-related model fiscal or licensing terms issued after the original Stabroek agreement. [S14]
7. Oil Pollution Prevention, Preparedness, Response and Responsibility Act 2025. [S53-S54]

Where a statute has not fully commenced, the final paper should identify which provisions are operative. Enactment should never be used as shorthand for implementation.

## A8. Permit and licence chronology

Petroleum projects should be tracked through separate administrative records:
- discovery well;
- appraisal;
- development application;
- EIA scoping;
- public notice;
- environmental permit;
- production licence;
- construction milestones;
- first oil;
- permit amendment;
- environmental audit;
- incident and enforcement history.

Liza Phase 1, Liza Phase 2, Payara, Yellowtail, Uaru and Whiptail should each have an individual row. [S48-S56, S86] Projects not yet producing at the evidence cut-off should retain blank actual-production fields.

This approach prevents a sanctioned project from being treated as an operating project. It also prevents environmental conditions from being generalized across different developments.

## A9. Judicial and dispute chronology

A resource-governance chronology should include litigation and contractual dispute processes, but the database should not equate an allegation with a final decision.

For court matters, record:
- court;
- case number;
- filing date;
- issue;
- interim order;
- trial judgment;
- appeal;
- appellate judgment;
- further appeal;
- final status;
- operative remedy.

For contract audits, record:
- audit period;
- auditor;
- amount reviewed;
- proposed adjustment;
- operator response;
- negotiation status;
- sole-expert or arbitration step;
- final determination;
- cash consequence.

Guyana's cost-audit records and the financial-assurance litigation illustrate both types. [S20, S42-S43, S71-S72] The final publication should use authenticated legal texts before stating the legal effect of the appellate decision.

## A10. Source hierarchy for dates

When dates conflict, use this hierarchy:
1. enacted statute or official gazette for legal events;
2. executed agreement for contract signature and obligations;
3. regulator or ministry licence register for administrative events;
4. audited financial statements for accounting events;
5. regulator production series for actual production;
6. operator announcement for project start where regulator data are unavailable;
7. media reporting only when the primary record is unavailable and the limitation is stated.

The hierarchy is not absolute. A later audited restatement may supersede an earlier official figure. Version history should therefore be preserved.

## A11. New-producer chronology template

A prospective producer should build the chronology before commercial discovery. The minimum columns are:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Event ID | Stable reference |
| Date type | Geological, contractual, statutory, administrative, operational, financial |
| Start date | Exact date or documented range |
| End date | If applicable |
| Instrument | Law, licence, agreement, permit, account or project |
| Institution | Responsible authority |
| Status | Proposed, enacted, commenced, approved, operating, superseded, disputed |
| Evidence | Primary source |
| Confidence | High, medium or unresolved based on source completeness |
| Dependency | Earlier condition that must be satisfied |
| Later amendment | Version-control link |
| Public URL | Reproducible access |

This template turns chronology into an audit tool.

## A12. Decision-window analysis

The Guyana case contains four decision windows.

**Window 1: before discovery.** The priority is legal clarity, environmental baseline science, data ownership and compact technical capability.

**Window 2: discovery to final investment decision.** The priority is independent commercial analysis, contract interpretation, environmental alternatives and fiscal design.

**Window 3: final investment decision to first oil.** The priority is revenue custody, metering, audit capacity, spill preparedness, public communication and budget rules.

**Window 4: scale-up.** The priority shifts toward cost verification, public investment quality, local value, macroeconomic stability, environmental compliance and statistical capacity.

The windows are more transferable than the exact Guyanese calendar. A country with a longer development period has more time to prepare. A country with a single nearshore discovery may need a different environmental sequence.

## A13. Chronology failure modes

Common errors include:
- applying later fiscal terms to an earlier agreement;
- describing a law as operational before commencement;
- using an operator's target as an actual production date;
- reporting a fund deposit as budget spending;
- attributing a pre-existing climate programme to petroleum;
- using a public announcement as evidence of completed infrastructure;
- treating a disputed audit adjustment as recovered revenue;
- combining a court order and an appellate reversal without version control.

The final publication's timeline should visibly flag each risk.

## A14. Chronology as a policy-transfer screen

Before transferring any instrument from Guyana, another country should ask:
1. At what stage did Guyana adopt it?
2. What problem existed at that time?
3. Which institutions already existed?
4. Was the instrument later amended?
5. What evidence shows implementation?
6. What remained contested or incomplete?
7. Does the receiving country face the same timing problem?

A rule adopted after first oil cannot be treated as proof of what must exist before first oil without explaining the difference. The purpose of the case study is to make timing explicit.

## A15. Annex conclusion

Guyana's value as a case study lies partly in the density of dated transitions. Exploration, discovery, contract negotiation, pre-oil fund preparation, first production, revised legislation, local-content rules, project expansion, environmental law and ongoing accountability reforms occurred within a relatively short period. That makes chronological discipline essential.

For a newcomer, the principal operational lesson is to build a version-controlled public chronology before revenue arrives. The chronology becomes the backbone for contract audit, environmental oversight, sovereign-fund accounting and later institutional review.
