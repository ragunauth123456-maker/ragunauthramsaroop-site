# FROM DISCOVERY TO DEVELOPMENT
## Guyana's petroleum transition, institutional choices and a replication handbook for emerging producers

**Research charter and publication architecture, 27 September 2026.** Independent research by Ragunauth Ramsaroop. Working title, not a finding that any jurisdiction is an unqualified best practice. Covers the pre-oil forest-policy period, petroleum exploration and discovery, first oil, operational scale-up and evidence available by September 2026.

**Research question.** Which actions in Guyana's exploration-to-production transition were taken before revenue arrived, how did they operate during production, where are the observable results and remaining gaps, and which design options are transferable to a small island or a new resource-producing state?

**Thesis to test, not presume.** Guyana offers an unusually informative sequence: a low-carbon forest-finance strategy preceded its major offshore discovery; a petroleum revenue fund was legislated before first oil; industrial production expanded quickly; statutory, environmental, local-content and fiscal institutions evolved during expansion. Timing is not sufficient evidence of quality. The study therefore measures delivery, disclosure, participation, fiscal resilience, spill readiness, opportunity costs and outcomes against dated source records.

### Evidence rules
- As-of date: 27 September 2026. All first-half 2026 figures are period-specific and never silently annualized. Distinguish operator capacity, measured daily production, cumulative barrels and operator forecasts.
- Record four evidence types: observed independently reported data, government/operator administrative reports, enacted law and contract text, and contested legal or stakeholder assertions. Mark projections separately.
- Every chart links to a source version, page/table, date, definition and reproducing calculation. A missing household survey is an evidence gap, not proof of universal household improvement.
- Compare institutions over equivalent stages of petroleum development. No governance rankings or political verdicts. Distinguish fiscal design, enforcement capacity and observed welfare outcomes.
- Environmental outcomes require baseline, monitoring methodology, permit compliance reports, independent inspections and response capability. A permit or an environmental assessment is not proof of zero harm.
- Extractive sector receipts, sovereign wealth fund market value, authorized withdrawals and annual spending are separate series; never aggregate them as though interchangeable.
- Quotes and third-party data stay attributed. No fabricated interviews, peer review, endorsements or confidential employer information. No access to K1 or other private computers.

## The central chronology to prove
- **2009, separate pre-oil thread:** Guyana finalised its original Low Carbon Development Strategy and entered a results-based forest partnership with Norway. The 2024 LCDS update reports US$227 million earned over 2009-2015. Treat the forest mechanism separately from petroleum revenue. [S09]
- **2015:** Commercial Liza discovery changed the expected value of Guyana's offshore petroleum rights. Confirm detailed licence and seismic chronology from official archives before publication. [S01]
- **2016:** Stabroek production-sharing agreement became the fiscal basis for the main producing projects. It provides a 2% royalty, up to 75% cost recovery and a 50:50 split of profit petroleum. Review the exact tax and cost-recovery schedules before presenting government-take estimates. [S03,S04]
- **January 2019:** The first Natural Resource Fund Act was assented to, before first oil. The 2021 Act later replaced it and assigned operational fund management to the Bank of Guyana. [S05]
- **20 December 2019:** ExxonMobil announced first oil from Liza Phase 1. The pre-revenue period between discovery and production provides the most relevant window for a new producer. [S01]
- **2021-2023:** 2021 NRF and Local Content Acts, 2022 LCDS 2030 parliamentary adoption and the 2023 Petroleum Activities Act changed the institutional framework. Avoid treating statutory creation as verified enforcement. [S05,S09,S12,S13]
- **February 2024:** Fiscal Enactments (Amendment) Act revised the NRF withdrawal ceiling. Evaluate changed saving/spending paths using the amended formula, not the original 2021 schedule. [S06]
- **August 2025:** Yellowtail became the fourth producing Stabroek project; operator-reported installed capacity exceeded 900,000 b/d. Actual sustained output is a different measure. [S02]
- **First half of 2026:** The September 2026 government mid-year release estimates 163.3 million barrels and approximately 902,000 b/d on average, and estimates the June NRF balance at US$4.2942 billion. Reconcile the financial balance against the June BOG report and distinguish estimates from audited results. [S07]
- **May 2026:** Court of Appeal reversed a lower-court order requiring an unlimited financial guarantee in the offshore environmental permit litigation. The scope of insurance, enforceable affiliate and parent guarantees, recovery time and modeled worst-case damages remain separate empirical questions. [S11]
- **May 2026:** EITI began a full Guyana Validation following Board concerns regarding stakeholder confidence in the independence of civil-society representative selection. Record the later decision when published, not a premature pass/fail conclusion. [S10]

## Original analytical contributions
1. **Two clocks:** one timeline follows discovery, final investment decision, first oil and production capacity; the second follows law, regulator staffing, fund establishment, environmental baseline, public engagement, audit and public reporting. Calculate each institutional lead or lag in months, then test whether an early statute produced early operational capability.
2. **Five-asset conversion ledger:** annual depletion of subsurface petroleum wealth alongside creation or maintenance of financial, human, physical, institutional and natural capital. Net national wealth must not be inferred from gross GDP growth or capital appropriation alone.
3. **Petroleum-to-budget bridge:** reconcile entitlement barrels, realized prices, royalties, cost oil, profit oil, deposits, financial returns, approved withdrawals, actual transfers, budget commitments and verified completed public assets.
4. **Dual carbon ledger:** distinguish forest carbon-credit accounting and cash receipts from the emissions arising from domestic operations, exported petroleum and downstream consumption. Do not claim forest credits erase fossil-fuel emissions.
5. **Environmental capacity test:** explicitly model the earliest response time, equipment availability, overlapping spills, shoreline exposure, fisheries disruption, cleanup and compensation under unfavorable winds/currents and contractor insolvency. An advertised financial guarantee does not prove immediate liquidity.
6. **New-producer readiness pack:** milestone-level project owners, competent authority map, legislation sequence, sample disclosure calendar, independently verified environmental baseline, templates for competitive procurement and public consultation.

## Main contested questions and fair treatment
**PSA terms.** The 2016 contract was signed amid pre-production geological and financing uncertainty. Analysis must show the risk-sharing case using information known in 2016 alongside later production results. Explain the fiscal opportunity cost of low royalty/high cost recovery without silently applying post-discovery hindsight or later model PSA terms to the original contract.

**NRF governance.** Establishment before production and published bank reporting are observable. Higher 2024 withdrawal ceilings alter the accumulation path and may increase investment capacity as well as expenditure/overheating exposure. Compare enacted controls, reported compliance, outside audit, project-level investment results and intergenerational savings under multiple oil-price paths.

**Environmental protection.** The May 2026 appellate decision addresses the legal interpretation of the permit and EPA authority, not a scientific determination of spill probability or maximum loss. Government, operator, coastal communities, fishers, environmental advocates and courts hold different relevant roles. Present dated positions separately from assessed environmental evidence.

**Transparency.** Published agreements and reports improve the raw information available for scrutiny. The EITI Board's May 2026 statement records concerns about stakeholder representation. Examine beneficial ownership, disputed disclosures, ordinary citizen access to machine-readable data and independent follow-up; do not equate membership with compliance.

**Forest finance and oil.** The forestry strategy predates the commercial oil discovery and should be studied as an independent institutional thread. Additionality, leakage, permanence, community decision authority and registered carbon transactions need separate checks. Parallel oil production requires transparent emissions disclosure.

**Social distribution.** Government allocations, aggregate growth and announced jobs are not household welfare outcomes. Do not make current poverty claims using a 2019 baseline as though it were 2026 measurement. Demand disaggregated household, coastal/hinterland, gender, Indigenous and youth evidence.

## International comparative design
- **Norway:** legal fund created in 1990 and first transfer made in 1996, well after oil discovery. Study fiscal-rule credibility, overseas investment and decades-long institutional maturation. Differences in prior administrative and tax capacity prevent a direct one-for-one transplant. [S16]
- **Ghana:** petroleum revenue law of 2011 includes government allocation and accountability mechanisms. Read the amended 2015 text and independent oversight evidence. Ghana is a comparison of post-discovery institutional sequencing, not an assumed failure or success. [S17]
- **Timor-Leste:** use a later verified central bank Petroleum Fund report for fiscal dependence and estimated sustainable income. Until source and current legal text are checked, the comparative chapter records research questions rather than empirical assertions.
- **Small islands and frontier producers:** use exposure-weighted adaptation for coastal risk, maritime response distance, land constraints, fiscal base, civil-service staffing and tourism/fisheries reliance. A sovereign fund cannot substitute for a functioning environmental regulator.

## A 24-month operational decision template for a first-time producer
| Time | Evidence and decision dossier | Accountable function | Verification |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 0-90 days | All existing licences/PSAs, beneficial owners, exploration commitments, environmental baselines and regulator capability map inventoried | Petroleum ministry, auditor and environment authority | Public contract inventory and gaps log |
| 0-90 days | Independent cost-recovery, economic and spill-finance legal review scoped | Finance ministry and regulator with conflict-screened advisers | Published terms of reference and independent review |
| 3-6 months | Revenue collection account and draft statutory withdrawal options prepared | Treasury, central bank, parliament | Scenario and legal audit, consultations |
| 3-6 months | Offshore spill simulation and community baseline designed before production authorisation | EPA, coast guard, fisheries agencies and coastal states | Exercise log, equipment checks and baseline study |
| 6-12 months | Public revenue dashboard, licence cadastre, EITI/other disclosure and procurement register available | Statistics office and independent oversight bodies | Reconciliation against payment and bank records |
| 6-12 months | Skills, supplier and anti-fronting tests specified with measured baseline | Local-content authority and training agencies | Third-party ownership and procurement sampling |
| 12-24 months | First-oil transition rehearsed including lifts, valuation, treasury booking and publication | Operators, treasury, central bank and auditor | Simulated monthly reconciliation |
| 12-24 months | Independent environmental drills and compensation route tested | Regulator, responders and communities | Corrective actions closed before permit condition signoff |
| Years 3-5 | Rolling five-year public investment and maintenance pipeline matched to volatile resource receipts | Budget agency and delivery units | Published appraisals, costs and project outcomes |

**Implementation choice, not policy verdict:** each jurisdiction should compare separate stabilization and intergenerational accounts, a single pooled fund, alternative withdrawal rules and independent fiscal councils in an open fiscal and constitutional process, recording both advantages and failure modes.
