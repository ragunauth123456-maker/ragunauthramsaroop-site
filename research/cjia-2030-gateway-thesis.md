# CJIA 2030 Gateway Thesis

Updated: 29 September 2026

Purpose: source dossier supporting the investor-facing LinkedIn/PDF brief on Cheddi Jagan International Airport (CJIA), Terminal Two and Guyana's aviation opportunity through 2030 and beyond.

## Executive evidence base

### 1. Terminal Two now has a defined financing and delivery framework
- The new CJIA Terminal Two/arrivals terminal is planned at approximately 150,000 sq ft.
- Government budget documents place the project period from 2025 through 2028.
- Budget 2026 states preparatory works commenced in 2025 and the terminal is slated for completion in Q1 2028.
- A EUR 61.43 million financing facility with Banco Santander was signed on 12 June 2026 and is covered by UK Export Finance.
- BHM Construction International (UK) Ltd is the exporter/contractor. Manchester Airports Group is collaborating on design and construction.
- Publicly stated commercial features include expanded duty-free, retail, rental offices, lounges, a functioning transit lounge, baggage upgrades and separation of arrivals/departures functions.
- Separate airport works include runway/taxiway reconstruction, air traffic control tower relocation, aircraft parking expansion, baggage systems, gates, VIP facilities and administrative upgrades.

### 2. CJIA traffic growth is already material before Terminal Two opens
Official data for 2020 to 2025:
- International aircraft landings: 1,646 to 5,827, +254%.
- Passenger movements: 182,736 to 973,016, +432.5%.
- Available seats: 341,506 to 1,708,095, +400.2%.
- Cargo imports and exports: 7.66 million kg to 14.43 million kg, +88.5%.
- 2024 to 2025 passenger growth: +13.6%.
- 2024 to 2025 seat growth: +14.8%.
- 2024 to 2025 cargo growth: +6.3%.

### 3. Government policy now explicitly targets a transit-hub model
On 29 September 2026, the Government stated that negotiations with international airlines interested in using Terminal Two as a hub were ready to be finalised. The stated route-development focus includes connecting West Africa and South America to the Caribbean through Guyana.

The Government also directed a review of aviation laws and regulations to align standards with international requirements as air traffic expands.

Sources:
- https://dpi.gov.gy/government-ready-to-finalise-deal-for-major-airline-to-use-cjia-as-global-hub/
- https://dpi.gov.gy/president-orders-review-of-all-aviation-laws/

### 4. Guyana has opened a legal pathway to direct African air service
Guyana and Morocco signed an Open Skies Air Services Agreement on 17 April 2026. The agreement covers market access, traffic rights, designation and authorisation, charges, licensing, customs, tariffs and commercial activities. The GCAA publicly stated that it looked forward to Royal Air Maroc becoming the first African airline to serve Guyana.

This is a regulatory enabler, not proof that a scheduled route is already confirmed.

Primary source:
- https://dpi.gov.gy/guyana-and-morocco-sign-open-skies-agreement/

### 5. The 2030 aviation target is distinct from the tourism target
The most current public position separates two ambitions:
- Three million passengers by 2030.
- One million visitors by 2030.

2025 visitor arrivals reached roughly 453,489. By the end of August 2026, 320,174 visitors had arrived and the Government was projecting more than 500,000 for full-year 2026.

### 6. Hotel capacity is expanding alongside airlift
Government statements during the 2026 Budget debate reported:
- 453,408 visitor arrivals in 2025.
- 738 hotel rooms added by new international brands in 2025.
- More than 1,000 additional rooms projected for 2026.
- Total room stock projected to exceed 5,600 by end-2026.

This supports the thesis that aviation growth is being matched by hospitality capacity, while execution and occupancy economics still require project-level diligence.

## Investment thesis

The strongest investor case is not "Terminal Two is being built." The stronger case is that four demand systems are advancing at the same time:

1. Airport capacity and transit infrastructure.
2. Passenger and visitor growth.
3. Corporate travel, oil-sector logistics and non-oil investment.
4. Bilateral air-service liberalisation and route development.

This combination creates commercial possibilities across:
- anchor airline or focus-city operations
- cargo and freight forwarding
- cold chain and time-sensitive logistics
- ground handling
- airline catering
- MRO and technical services
- aviation training
- lounges and premium passenger services
- duty free and retail
- airport hotels and hospitality
- parking and ground transport
- digital identity, passenger processing and security systems
- airport real estate and support services

## What must not be overstated

- A signed Open Skies agreement does not equal a launched route.
- A government passenger target is not a forecast of guaranteed demand.
- Terminal completion timing should be stated as Q1 2028 based on Budget 2026, while later project updates should be monitored.
- Guyana's hub thesis must be tested against connection-bank economics, fleet range, local origin-and-destination demand, airport charges, transfer security, minimum connection times, bilateral rights and airline network strategy.
- The airport's scale remains smaller than established regional hubs. The opportunity is an emerging gateway thesis, not current hub dominance.
- Investor returns depend on concession terms, traffic capture, capex, regulation, labour, FX, competitive response and execution quality.
