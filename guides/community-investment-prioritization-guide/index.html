Community Investment Prioritization Guide

How to compare community investment options using evidence, impact, urgency, reach and feasibility.

How to compare community investment options using evidence, impact, urgency, reach and feasibility. The useful output is not a single number or ranking. The useful output is a transparent record of assumptions, evidence gaps and the next validation step.

Use the tool as a screen. Keep the inputs editable, preserve source dates and move material decisions to the appropriate professional, regulatory or organisational review.

Recommended sequence

  1. Start with community-defined evidence rather than an internal project list.
  2. Rate expected impact and urgency separately from reach.
  3. Treat feasibility as a delivery constraint rather than a reason to ignore high-impact needs.
  4. Validate the ranking with affected communities, rights-holders and programme owners before funding decisions.

What to record before sharing a result

Record the reporting period, source of each material input, the definition used for key terms, the methodology version and the limitations that remain unresolved. A result without this context is easier to misread.
