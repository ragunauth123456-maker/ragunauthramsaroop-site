ESG Readiness Assessment Guide

How to use an ESG readiness assessment without turning it into a vanity score

An ESG readiness assessment should expose gaps in evidence, accountability and management systems. It should not reward polished language or imply that one composite score establishes sustainability performance.

Begin with governance. An organisation should be able to identify who owns each material issue, what evidence is reviewed, how decisions are escalated and which commitments have been approved.

Then test operating evidence. Climate, environment, workforce, human rights, communities, ethics, supply chain and reporting should be assessed with documents, data and controls rather than aspiration alone.

Low scores are useful when they trigger action. A readiness assessment becomes practical when each weak area has an owner, an evidence requirement, a measurable action and a review date.

High scores also need challenge. Ask whether the evidence is current, whether controls work in practice, whether affected stakeholders would describe performance the same way and whether disclosed claims match operating reality.
